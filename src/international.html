For Applicants Outside the United States

The International Applicant Guide

Roughly one in three students at a top US MBA program holds a passport from somewhere else. This page collects the rules that decide whether the degree pays off: how long you are admitted for, how long you can work afterward, and the single admissions question that doubles or triples your working time in the United States. Every number below cites the government document it came from.

What Changed in 2026

Effective September 15, 2026

The Department of Homeland Security replaced duration of status for F, J, and I nonimmigrants with admission for a fixed time period. F and J students are now admitted for the length of the program, not to exceed 4 years. Staying beyond that period requires an approved extension of stay rather than simply remaining enrolled.

The rule is classified as a major rule subject to congressional review. DHS states that if the effective date changes at the conclusion of that review, it will publish a further document to establish the actual date or terminate the rule.

Source: Department of Homeland Security, "Establishing a Fixed Time Period of Admission and an Extension of Stay Procedure for Nonimmigrant Academic Students, Exchange Visitors, and Representatives of Foreign Information Media," final rule, 91 FR 44976, published July 17, 2026, effective September 15, 2026. Official text at govinfo.gov.

Also Pending, Not Yet Law

On September 11, 2026 DHS published a proposed rule to remove 8 CFR 214.1(l)(2), the discretionary grace period of up to 60 days that certain nonimmigrants receive when employment ends before their authorized stay expires. This is a notice of proposed rulemaking, not a final rule: written comments are due on or before November 10, 2026, and nothing has changed yet. It matters later, when an MBA graduate is working on H-1B and a job ends.

Source: Department of Homeland Security, "Eliminating the Discretionary 60-Day Grace Period," notice of proposed rulemaking, 91 FR 57807, published September 11, 2026, comments close November 10, 2026. Official text at govinfo.gov.

The STEM MBA Question

This is the highest-leverage question an international applicant can ask an admissions office, and most applicants never ask it. A degree that appears on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List qualifies its graduate for a 24-month extension of post-completion OPT. A degree that does not gets 12 months and stops.

The Fact That Surprises People

CIP code 52.0201, "Business Administration and Management, General," the code a conventional MBA carries, is not on the DHS STEM list. Being at a famous school does not change this. What changes it is the CIP code your specific program is registered under in SEVIS.

Every Business Series Code on the DHS STEM List

The list designates four primary CIP series at the two-digit level plus named codes elsewhere. These are the only codes in the 52 series, "Business, Management, Marketing and Related Support Services," that appear on it:

What to Ask Admissions, Word for Word

Send this to every program on your shortlist. The answer is a fact the school already knows, and a school that will not answer it in writing is telling you something.

"Which CIP code is your full-time MBA registered under in SEVIS, and does that code currently appear on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List? If some concentrations or tracks carry a different CIP code than the general MBA, which ones, and what are their codes?"

Three answers are common. The program is coded 52.0201 and is not STEM. The whole MBA is coded to a STEM code and every graduate qualifies. Or, most often, the general MBA is not STEM but specific tracks are, in which case your concentration choice on day one determines your work authorization on graduation day.

Post-Completion OPT

  • Up to 12 months of employment authorization, available before completing studies (pre-completion), after completing them (post-completion), or split between the two. Time used pre-completion reduces post-completion eligibility.
  • Pre-completion OPT is limited to 20 hours or less per week while school is in session, and may be full time when school is not in session, after one full academic year of lawful enrollment.
  • On post-completion OPT you must work part time of at least 20 hours per week, or full time.
  • You may apply up to 90 days before you complete your degree and no later than 60 days after, and you must apply within 30 days after your designated school official enters the OPT recommendation into your SEVIS record.

Source: US Citizenship and Immigration Services, "Optional Practical Training (OPT) for F-1 Students." uscis.gov. Retrieved {{TODAY}}.

The 24-Month STEM Extension

  • A 24-month extension of post-completion OPT, for a combined maximum of 36 months.
  • Requires a degree in a field on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List, an employer enrolled in E-Verify, and a completed Form I-983 training plan.
  • Unemployment: up to 90 days during the initial period, plus an additional 60 days during the extension, for a combined total of 150 days.
  • File Form I-765 up to 90 days before current OPT authorization expires and within 60 days of the DSO's SEVIS recommendation. If filed on time and your OPT expires while the application is pending, employment authorization is extended automatically for 180 days.
  • A graduate who earns a qualifying bachelor's degree, uses a 24-month extension, and later earns a qualifying master's degree may apply for an additional 24-month extension on the master's.

Source: US Citizenship and Immigration Services, "Optional Practical Training Extension for STEM Students (STEM OPT)." uscis.gov. Retrieved {{TODAY}}.

The H-1B Reality

OPT is temporary. For most international MBA graduates who intend to stay, the next step is an H-1B petition filed by an employer, and the H-1B is capped by statute.

65,000
Regular Annual Cap
New H-1B statuses or visas each fiscal year.
20,000
US Master's Exemption
Additional petitions for beneficiaries with a master's or higher from a US institution.

A US MBA counts as a US master's degree for the additional 20,000 allocation, which is a genuine advantage of studying in the United States rather than elsewhere. Registration is electronic: USCIS will not consider a cap-subject petition properly filed "unless it is based on a valid, selected registration." Higher education institutions, affiliated nonprofits, and nonprofit or government research organizations are exempt from the numerical cap, which is why university and research employers can hire outside the lottery calendar.

Source: US Citizenship and Immigration Services, "H-1B Specialty Occupations." uscis.gov. Retrieved {{TODAY}}.

Why the STEM Question Decides This

The H-1B registration window opens once a year. A graduate with 12 months of OPT gets one registration before the clock runs out. A graduate with 36 months of OPT gets three. Nothing else in the application process moves the odds this much, and it is settled by a four-digit code chosen before you enroll.

Fees and Paperwork

The I-901 SEVIS Fee

Once a school admits you and issues a Form I-20, you pay the I-901 SEVIS fee before your visa interview. The fee for F and M visa applicants is $350, paid in full. You pay it using the SEVIS ID number from your I-20 together with your last name and date of birth, online by credit card or by check, money order, or Western Union. The printable payment confirmation replaces the mailed I-797C receipt.

Source: US Immigration and Customs Enforcement, "I-901 SEVIS Fee." ice.gov. Retrieved {{TODAY}}. This fee is separate from the visa application fee charged by the Department of State, and separate from any school deposit.

English Requirements

Two rules of thumb hold across US MBA programs, and both have exceptions you have to check school by school rather than trust from a forum post:

  • Most programs accept TOEFL iBT, IELTS Academic, or the Duolingo English Test, and set a minimum overall score, sometimes with minimums per section. The per-section minimum is what catches people out: a strong overall score with a weak Speaking band can still fall short.
  • Many programs waive the English requirement for applicants who completed a degree taught in English, or who have worked for a set period in an English-language environment. The definition of "taught in English" is the school's, not a general standard.

We do not publish a table of per-school minimums here, because these change yearly and a stale minimum is worse than none. Open the program page for each school on your shortlist and read its own admissions requirements; the school pages in our MBA rankings link to the official program site for every school we track.

Building a Shortlist From Outside the United States

Applying from abroad adds three filters on top of the ones a domestic applicant uses. Our rankings carry the underlying data with a source on every figure, so you can apply these yourself:

  • International share of the class. A program where a third or more of the class is international has admissions readers who are used to foreign transcripts, foreign employers, and foreign grading scales. Our school table publishes each program's international percentage with the class profile it came from.
  • The CIP code. Ask before you apply, using the wording above. Record the answer next to each school.
  • Employment reporting. Look for programs that publish employment outcomes broken out in a way you can actually read. A median salary with no reporting rate behind it tells you less than a lower median with 90 percent of the class reporting.
