Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 17, 2026. This policy covers paid plans, which are now live, the consent banner that gates site analytics, and how long each kind of record is kept.

1. The Short Version

  • You can use the trainer without an account. In that mode your practice data lives only in your browser's local storage and never reaches our servers.
  • If you create an account, we sync your practice data so you can move between devices. We use it to run the product, not to advertise to you.
  • The free plan never asks for a card. We only ever see payment details if you choose a paid plan, and even then the card itself goes to Stripe and never to us.
  • Site analytics only run if you say yes. The first time you arrive, a banner asks. Rejecting is one click in the same place as accepting, the site behaves identically either way, and you can change your mind from Privacy Choices in the footer of any page.
  • One thing we record always, because it is not about you: which practice question was answered, which option was chosen, whether it was right, and how many seconds it took. Those rows carry no account, no session, no device, and no address, so they cannot be traced to a person. They are how the practice engine learns which questions work.
  • We run no third-party ad trackers. On accounts recorded as 18 or over we may buy details about you from data partners and attach them to your profile, kept separate from what you told us and deleted with your account; nothing about anyone under 18 is ever bought. We also operate a data sharing programme that is open only to adults who opt in: if you are 18 or over and you switch it on, we may share the profile details listed in section 9 with partners, including data brokers. It is off unless you turn it on, you can turn it off at any time, and it is closed entirely to anyone under 18.

2. What We Collect

  • Account data. Your email address, used for passwordless sign-in links and essential service messages.
  • Practice data. Your answers, timing per question, skill ratings, review schedule, flashcard progress, session history, and settings such as your goal and test date.
  • Product events. Basic usage events (for example: round started, question answered, round completed) tied to your account, used to understand and improve the product.
  • Site analytics, only with your consent. If you accept, our pages run our own first-party measurement: page viewed, referrer, any campaign tags in the link you followed, device type, viewport width, time on page, a random session identifier that lives in your browser tab and clears when it closes, and which of five product milestones you reached in that visit: opening a trainer, answering a question, finishing a round, asking for a sign-in link, or opening checkout. Those milestones record the name of the step and nothing else, once per visit; they are how we can tell that people open the trainer and never answer anything, which is a thing we could not see before and cannot fix without seeing. Your IP address is used in transit to derive a country code and a salted one-way hash for abuse prevention; the raw IP address is never stored in our database. Nothing in this bullet is collected before you choose, nothing is collected at all if you decline, and if your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal we treat that as a decline without asking. We use no advertising cookies and no third-party trackers. None of what this bullet describes is ever shared with a broker or a partner: the data sharing programme in section 9 covers self-declared profile details only, and analytics records are not part of it.
  • Item telemetry, which is not personal data. Every time a practice question is answered anywhere on the site we record the exam, the question identifier, its skill, section and difficulty, which option was chosen, whether it was correct, how many seconds it took, which mode it was answered in, how long it took before any option was picked, how many times the answer was switched before submitting, and, when you tell us, that you were guessing and which of our fixed reasons explains the miss. That record deliberately carries no account identifier, no session identifier, no device identifier and no address, and there is no column in which any of those could be stored, so a row cannot be linked back to you or to any other row you produced. Because it identifies nobody it is not gated behind the consent banner, and the banner says so rather than hiding it. It is the only evidence we have about which questions are ambiguous, which wrong answers are tempting for the right reasons, and where the engine should send you next. We do not record keystrokes, mouse movement, screen recordings, or anything you type into an answer box or an essay.
  • Birth month and year. Asked once when you create an account, for one purpose: working out whether you are 18, which decides whether the data sharing programme in section 9 is offered to you at all and whether you can buy a paid plan. We deliberately do not store the day. A full date of birth is one of the three fields that re-identify most people on their own, and the month and year answer the only question we need to ask. You can correct it from the Account page; correcting it downward past 18 revokes any sharing opt-in automatically.
  • Optional profile details. If you choose to fill them in on the Account page we store your age range, gender, country, role (student, educator, and so on), education level, intended major, target schools, score goal, application year, budget band, employer industry, household income band, first-generation status, military status, and weekly study hours. Every one is optional, self-reported, editable, and blank until you type in it. We never infer any of them from your behaviour, and nothing we buy from a third party is ever written into them; purchased details are held separately and described below. They are used in aggregate to understand who we serve, and, if and only if you are 18 or over and have switched on the programme in section 9, they are the fields that programme can share. None of it is shown publicly.
  • Community forum. Posts are public by design, whether made under a name or the anonymous handle your browser generates. We store the post, the name you chose, a country code, and the salted IP hash above (for spam and abuse control). Anonymous posts are not linked to an account.
  • Error reports. When a page of ours throws a JavaScript error, the browser sends us the error message, the file and line it came from, the stack trace, the page you were on, the build version, your device type, and the same random session identifier the analytics beacon uses. It is how we find out that something is broken before you have to tell us. It is first party, it goes nowhere except our own database, and no third-party error service is involved. We cap what is stored and we do not put your practice answers or your email address into these reports.
  • Details bought about you, on adult accounts only. If your account is recorded as 18 or over, we may buy attributes about you from data partners and commercial data providers and attach them to your profile. The categories are the same as the ones we ask you for directly: education, field of study, career stage and industry, household financial band, and where you are in an application cycle. We buy nothing about anyone under 18, and an account whose age is later corrected below 18 has everything bought about it deleted automatically rather than on request. Purchased values are held in their own table and are never written into the fields you filled in, so that what you told us and what we were sold about you stay distinguishable: to you, to us, and to a deletion request. Every row records which partner it came from and when, and expires rather than being kept indefinitely. Ask what is attached to your account at privacy@startfromnowhere.com and we will send it or delete it, your choice. None of it is used to decide your price, your access, or anything about your account, and we never use it, or let a recipient use it, for any decision about credit, employment, housing or insurance.
  • Payment and subscription data. Only if you start a paid plan. Your card number, expiry, and security code go directly to Stripe on Stripe's own checkout pages; they never pass through our site and we never store them. What we store against your account is the tier you are on (Free, Plus, or Pro), whether it is trialing, active, past due, or cancelled, the dates your trial and billing period end, whether a cancellation is pending, the price and billing interval you signed up at, and the Stripe customer and subscription identifiers that let us look your subscription up. Stripe holds the payment method, the charge history, and the receipts; you can see all of it through Manage Billing on your account page.

3. How We Use It

  • To take payment and run your subscription, if you have one: to start the trial, charge the renewal, show you the next billing date, and act on a cancellation.
  • To run the adaptive engine: your answers drive your skill ratings, question selection, and review schedule.
  • To sync your progress across devices when you are signed in.
  • To fix problems and improve question quality, using aggregate statistics such as per-question correct rates and which wrong option a question attracts most.
  • To understand usage trends in aggregate (which pages, features, and study modes help people most) so we can build what actually works. Individual browsing is reviewed only for security, abuse, or support reasons.
  • To send the service emails you request, such as sign-in links. Optional email like a weekly digest has its own toggle in your account.
  • To share self-declared profile details with partners and data brokers, but only where you are 18 or over and have switched the programme in section 9 on. This is the one use on this list that is off by default, the one that requires a separate yes, and the one you can withdraw at any time.

4. Where It Lives and Who Processes It

  • Supabase (hosted in the United States) stores synced account data, protected by row-level security so each account can only read its own rows.
  • Cloudflare serves the website and the app.
  • Stripe processes payments through Stripe-hosted checkout and the Stripe billing portal. Stripe receives your email address and your card details and acts as the controller of that payment data under its own privacy policy. We receive back only the subscription facts listed in section 2.
  • Outside the opt-in programme described in section 9, we do not sell or rent personal data, and we share it only with the processors above or where the law requires it. Practice answers, essays and anything you type into the trainer are never part of the programme.
  • Where your data goes. These providers are based in the United States, so if you use the Services from outside the US your data is transferred there and handled under US law. We rely on standard contractual clauses or the equivalent transfer mechanism each provider offers.

5. Who Can See It Inside Start From Nowhere

  • Access is limited to accounts on an explicit admin list held in the database, and every administrative query checks that list on the server before returning a row.
  • Administrators mostly work with aggregates: totals, country counts, per-question correct rates, traffic by page. That is what the dashboard is built around.
  • An administrator can also open a single account to support it or investigate abuse. That view shows the email address, sign-up and last sign-in dates, plan, exam and goal, any optional profile details you gave us, per-section and per-skill accuracy, recent practice sessions, and forum activity. It does not show payment card details, because we do not have them.
  • We look at an individual account only for support, security, abuse, or a legal obligation. We do not browse accounts out of curiosity and we do not use individual data to target advertising.

  • California. We have never sold or shared any other category, we do not sell or share the personal information of anyone under 18 at all, and we do not use or disclose sensitive personal information for any purpose beyond those permitted without a right to limit.   • India. You can access, correct, and erase your personal data, nominate someone to exercise your rights if you cannot, and raise a grievance with us at privacy@startfromnowhere.com. We do not run behavioural tracking or targeted advertising, and we do not direct advertising at children.
  • Making a request. Email privacy@startfromnowhere.com from your account address. We answer within 30 days and we do not charge for it.

8. Retention

Site analytics are kept for 400 days, and error reports for 90. Both carry a session identifier and a salted address hash, which makes them the records here that most clearly relate to a person, so they are the ones with the shortest useful life. Four hundred days on analytics is one full application cycle plus the same month a year earlier, which is the shortest window in which a comparison means anything for an admissions calendar. Ninety days on error reports is generous: a stack trace names a build version, and a trace from a build nobody is running any more is not evidence of anything. Both are deleted by a scheduled job, not by somebody remembering.

Item telemetry, the records described in section 2 that are not about you, is kept for 400 days and then deleted automatically. Those records identify nobody, which is why we do not ask your permission for them; that is not a reason to keep them forever, and a reading more than a year old describes a version of a question we have since edited.

We keep account and practice data while your account exists. If you delete your account, synced data is removed from the production database promptly and from backups on their rotation schedule. Local browser data is yours to clear at any time.

Payment records are the one exception. Tax and accounting law requires us, and requires Stripe, to keep a record of what was charged and to whom for several years after the transaction, so deleting your account does not erase the invoice history. That record is limited to the transaction itself; it does not include your practice data.

9. The Data Sharing Programme

It is off unless you switch it on. Nothing described here happens to your data unless you are 18 or over and you have opted in from the Account page. Declining costs you nothing: every feature, including the free tier, behaves identically either way.

Who it is open to. Adults only, 18 and over, verified by the birth month and year you give when you create an account. We do not store your birth date, only the month and the year, which is enough to work out your age and nothing more. If your age puts you under 18, the programme is not offered, and if an account that had opted in is later corrected to an age under 18 the opt-in is revoked automatically at the database level rather than by anybody remembering to do it.

What can be shared. Only the profile details you have chosen to fill in: education level, intended major, target schools, score goal, application year, budget band, employer industry, household income band, first-generation status, military status, study hours and which exam you are preparing for, together with your country. Every one of those is optional and self-declared. We never infer them from your behaviour. Where we have bought details about you from a data partner, as described in section 2, those are held separately from the fields above and may be shared alongside them while this is switched on.

What can never be shared, whatever you opt into. Your practice answers and ratings. Anything you type into an answer box or an essay. Your email address, name or payment details. Your IP address. Your error reports. Your forum posts.

Who it goes to. Partners in education, admissions, test preparation and student finance, which may include data brokers. Every recipient is under contract to use the data only for the purpose disclosed and not to re-identify you.

Turning it off. One switch on the Account page, and the Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information link in the footer of every page, which works whether or not you have an account. We honour a Global Privacy Control signal from your browser as an opt-out without asking you to do anything else. Opting out stops future sharing; it cannot recall a copy already delivered, which is why the choice is yours to make before rather than after.

What came before. Data collected before this policy took effect was collected while we told you we would never sell it. That data is excluded from the programme on its own terms, and the exclusion is enforced by the database rather than by a promise: the only view any export can read from filters on the date your consent was recorded against the version of this policy that disclosed the programme.

A record is kept. Every time you switch this on or off we store what you chose, when, and which version of this policy was in force, so that the answer to "did I agree to this" is a record rather than an argument.

10. Children and Students Under 18

The SAT and ACT trainers are for high school students, so we expect people under 18 to use the Services. If you are under 18, use them with a parent or guardian's permission. We collect the same practice data for a student under 18 as for anyone else, we do not use it for advertising, and we do not sell it, share it with brokers, buy anything about a student under 18, or build an advertising profile from it under any circumstances. The data sharing programme in section 9 is closed to you and cannot be switched on. That is not a setting we offer and then police: an account whose age is under 18 cannot appear in the only view an export is allowed to read. Only an adult can buy a paid plan or enter payment details.

We do not knowingly create accounts for children under 13 or knowingly collect their personal information. If you believe a child under 13 has an account, email privacy@startfromnowhere.com and we will delete the account and its data.
