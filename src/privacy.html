Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 17, 2026. This policy covers paid plans, which are now live, the consent banner that gates site analytics, and how long each kind of record is kept.

1. The Short Version

  • You can use the trainer without an account. In that mode your practice data lives only in your browser's local storage and never reaches our servers.
  • If you create an account, we sync your practice data so you can move between devices. We use it to run the product, not to advertise to you.
  • The free plan never asks for a card. We only ever see payment details if you choose a paid plan, and even then the card itself goes to Stripe and never to us.
  • Site analytics only run if you say yes. The first time you arrive, a banner asks. Rejecting is one click in the same place as accepting, the site behaves identically either way, and you can change your mind from Privacy Choices in the footer of any page.
  • One thing we record always, because it is not about you: which practice question was answered, which option was chosen, whether it was right, and how many seconds it took. Those rows carry no account, no session, no device, and no address, so they cannot be traced to a person. They are how the practice engine learns which questions work.
  • We do not sell personal data and we do not run third-party ad trackers.

2. What We Collect

  • Account data. Your email address, used for passwordless sign-in links and essential service messages.
  • Practice data. Your answers, timing per question, skill ratings, review schedule, flashcard progress, session history, and settings such as your goal and test date.
  • Product events. Basic usage events (for example: round started, question answered, round completed) tied to your account, used to understand and improve the product.
  • Site analytics, only with your consent. If you accept, our pages run our own first-party measurement: page viewed, referrer, any campaign tags in the link you followed, device type, viewport width, time on page, and a random session identifier that lives in your browser tab and clears when it closes. Your IP address is used in transit to derive a country code and a salted one-way hash for abuse prevention; the raw IP address is never stored in our database. Nothing in this bullet is collected before you choose, nothing is collected at all if you decline, and if your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal we treat that as a decline without asking. We use no advertising cookies, no third-party trackers, and we never sell or share personal data with data brokers.
  • Item telemetry, which is not personal data. Every time a practice question is answered anywhere on the site we record the exam, the question identifier, its skill, section and difficulty, which option was chosen, whether it was correct, how many seconds it took, which mode it was answered in, how long it took before any option was picked, how many times the answer was switched before submitting, and, when you tell us, that you were guessing and which of our fixed reasons explains the miss. That record deliberately carries no account identifier, no session identifier, no device identifier and no address, and there is no column in which any of those could be stored, so a row cannot be linked back to you or to any other row you produced. Because it identifies nobody it is not gated behind the consent banner, and the banner says so rather than hiding it. It is the only evidence we have about which questions are ambiguous, which wrong answers are tempting for the right reasons, and where the engine should send you next. We do not record keystrokes, mouse movement, screen recordings, or anything you type into an answer box or an essay.
  • Optional profile details. If you choose to tell us your age range, gender, country, or role (student, educator, and so on) on the Account page, we store it. Every one of these fields is optional, self-reported, editable, and used only in aggregate to understand who we serve and improve the product; none of it is shown publicly.
  • Community forum. Posts are public by design, whether made under a name or the anonymous handle your browser generates. We store the post, the name you chose, a country code, and the salted IP hash above (for spam and abuse control). Anonymous posts are not linked to an account.
  • Error reports. When a page of ours throws a JavaScript error, the browser sends us the error message, the file and line it came from, the stack trace, the page you were on, the build version, your device type, and the same random session identifier the analytics beacon uses. It is how we find out that something is broken before you have to tell us. It is first party, it goes nowhere except our own database, and no third-party error service is involved. We cap what is stored and we do not put your practice answers or your email address into these reports.
  • Payment and subscription data. Only if you start a paid plan. Your card number, expiry, and security code go directly to Stripe on Stripe's own checkout pages; they never pass through our site and we never store them. What we store against your account is the tier you are on (Free, Plus, or Pro), whether it is trialing, active, past due, or cancelled, the dates your trial and billing period end, whether a cancellation is pending, the price and billing interval you signed up at, and the Stripe customer and subscription identifiers that let us look your subscription up. Stripe holds the payment method, the charge history, and the receipts; you can see all of it through Manage Billing on your account page.

3. How We Use It

  • To take payment and run your subscription, if you have one: to start the trial, charge the renewal, show you the next billing date, and act on a cancellation.
  • To run the adaptive engine: your answers drive your skill ratings, question selection, and review schedule.
  • To sync your progress across devices when you are signed in.
  • To fix problems and improve question quality, using aggregate statistics such as per-question correct rates and which wrong option a question attracts most.
  • To understand usage trends in aggregate (which pages, features, and study modes help people most) so we can build what actually works. Individual browsing is reviewed only for security, abuse, or support reasons.
  • To send the service emails you request, such as sign-in links. Optional email like a weekly digest has its own toggle in your account.

4. Where It Lives and Who Processes It

  • Supabase (hosted in the United States) stores synced account data, protected by row-level security so each account can only read its own rows.
  • Cloudflare serves the website and the app.
  • Stripe processes payments through Stripe-hosted checkout and the Stripe billing portal. Stripe receives your email address and your card details and acts as the controller of that payment data under its own privacy policy. We receive back only the subscription facts listed in section 2.
  • We do not sell or rent personal data, and we share it only with the processors above or where the law requires it.
  • Where your data goes. These providers are based in the United States, so if you use the Services from outside the US your data is transferred there and handled under US law. We rely on standard contractual clauses or the equivalent transfer mechanism each provider offers.

5. Who Can See It Inside Start From Nowhere

  • Access is limited to accounts on an explicit admin list held in the database, and every administrative query checks that list on the server before returning a row.
  • Administrators mostly work with aggregates: totals, country counts, per-question correct rates, traffic by page. That is what the dashboard is built around.
  • An administrator can also open a single account to support it or investigate abuse. That view shows the email address, sign-up and last sign-in dates, plan, exam and goal, any optional profile details you gave us, per-section and per-skill accuracy, recent practice sessions, and forum activity. It does not show payment card details, because we do not have them.
  • We look at an individual account only for support, security, abuse, or a legal obligation. We do not browse accounts out of curiosity and we do not use individual data to target advertising.

6. Your Controls

  • Privacy choices. Accept or reject site analytics from the banner on your first visit, and change that decision at any time from Privacy Choices in the footer of every page. Your choice is stored in your own browser, so clearing site data resets it and you will be asked again. A Global Privacy Control signal from your browser is honoured as a rejection.
  • Export. Download your complete practice data from the Account page at any time.
  • Import or stay local. You can keep everything in your browser and never create an account.
  • Delete. Delete your account and synced data from the Account page. Deletion is permanent.
  • Cancel a paid plan. Account, then Plan and Billing, then Cancel Plan. One click, no email required.
  • Questions. Write to privacy@startfromnowhere.com and we will respond within 30 days. For anything about a charge or a subscription, write to billing@startfromnowhere.com.

7. Your Rights Where You Live

Wherever you are, you can use the controls above to see, export, or delete your data. Some places give you further rights in law, and we honour them whether or not they apply to you by right.

  • Europe and the United Kingdom. You can ask for access, correction, deletion, restriction, or a portable copy, and you can object to a use. We process account and practice data to perform our agreement with you; site analytics on your consent, which you give or refuse in the banner and can withdraw at any time from Privacy Choices in the footer; abuse prevention and item telemetry on our legitimate interest in running a working product, noting that item telemetry identifies nobody and so is not personal data at all; and optional profile details on your consent, which you can withdraw by clearing those fields. You can complain to your national data protection authority.
  • California. You can request the categories and specific pieces of personal information we hold, request deletion, and request correction. We do not sell or share personal information as those terms are defined in California law, and we will not discriminate against you for exercising a right.
  • India. You can access, correct, and erase your personal data, nominate someone to exercise your rights if you cannot, and raise a grievance with us at privacy@startfromnowhere.com. We do not run behavioural tracking or targeted advertising, and we do not direct advertising at children.
  • Making a request. Email privacy@startfromnowhere.com from your account address. We answer within 30 days and we do not charge for it.

8. Retention

Item telemetry, the records described in section 2 that are not about you, is kept for 400 days and then deleted automatically. Those records identify nobody, which is why we do not ask your permission for them; that is not a reason to keep them forever, and a reading more than a year old describes a version of a question we have since edited.

We keep account and practice data while your account exists. If you delete your account, synced data is removed from the production database promptly and from backups on their rotation schedule. Local browser data is yours to clear at any time.

Payment records are the one exception. Tax and accounting law requires us, and requires Stripe, to keep a record of what was charged and to whom for several years after the transaction, so deleting your account does not erase the invoice history. That record is limited to the transaction itself; it does not include your practice data.

9. Children and Students Under 18

The SAT trainer is for high school students, so we expect people under 18 to use the Services. If you are under 18, use them with a parent or guardian's permission. We collect the same practice data for a student under 18 as for anyone else, we do not use it for advertising, and we do not sell it. Only an adult can buy a paid plan or enter payment details.

We do not knowingly create accounts for children under 13 or knowingly collect their personal information. If you believe a child under 13 has an account, email privacy@startfromnowhere.com and we will delete the account and its data.

10. Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, we will note it here with a new date and, for signed-in users, flag it in the app before the change takes effect.
