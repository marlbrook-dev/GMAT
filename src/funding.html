Paying For It

How to Pay for an MBA After Grad PLUS Ended

Federal graduate borrowing changed on July 1, 2026, and the change is large enough to alter where people apply. Every figure below cites a primary source, and the one thing we could not verify is named rather than guessed.

The Short Version

  • Grad PLUS is gone for new borrowers. The Department of Education states the Act "eliminates the Grad PLUS program" effective July 1, 2026 (US Department of Education fact sheet, 2026).
  • Federal borrowing is now capped. Graduate students: $20,500 a year, $100,000 aggregate. Professional students: $50,000 a year, $200,000 aggregate (same source).
  • Already enrolled? You may be exempt. An interim exception applies to students enrolled as of June 30, 2026 who had already received a Direct Loan for that program.
  • This makes scholarships decisive. When federal loans no longer stretch to cover a full cost of attendance, the difference between a school that awards automatically and one that requires a separate application stops being a detail.

Is an MBA a Graduate Program or a Professional One?

This is the question that decides whether your federal cap is $20,500 a year or $50,000, and we could not verify the answer. Federal Student Aid's FAQ states that "the definition of professional student may be found in § 685.102(b)," and the classification was still moving through negotiated rulemaking when this page was written.

Three primary sources that would settle it refused this environment: studentaid.gov returned a 503, congress.gov returned a 403 on the Congressional Research Service analysis of the proposed definition, and ecfr.gov redirected to an unblock page. The 2024 edition of the regulation on govinfo predates the change and does not carry the new definition.

So: confirm your own program's classification with its financial aid office before you assume a number. Anyone telling you confidently which one an MBA is, without citing the current regulation, is guessing.

Veterans and Military: the Post-9/11 GI Bill

For August 1, 2026 through July 31, 2027, VA states it pays "the net tuition and mandatory fees" at a public institution, and at a private or foreign institution "the net tuition and mandatory fees up to $30,908.34" (VA Post-9/11 GI Bill rates, 2026).

What share of that you receive depends on qualifying active duty service:

Yellow Ribbon, and Why It Matters at a Private School

A top MBA costs well beyond the private-school cap, and Yellow Ribbon is the mechanism that closes the gap. VA states: "Your school will contribute a certain amount toward your extra tuition and fees through a grant, scholarship, or similar program. We'll match the contribution" (VA Yellow Ribbon Program, 2026).

Three things decide whether you actually get it, and all three are easy to miss:

  • You need the 100 percent benefit level. Below that, you do not qualify.
  • Each school caps how many students it funds under its agreement with VA, and places are first come, first served. Applying early is not a nicety here.
  • You have to ask. Get your Certificate of Eligibility, take it to the school's certifying official or financial aid office, and request the Yellow Ribbon Program. It is not automatic.

Several programs in our library name a Yellow Ribbon or veterans award directly, including MIT Sloan's Veterans Fellowship and the Yellow Ribbon participation listed by Chicago Booth and Stanford GSB. Check the rankings library for what each one publishes.

Scholarships From the Schools Themselves

With federal borrowing capped, school money is the largest lever most applicants have. The single most actionable fact is whether a school reviews every admit automatically or requires a separate application, because the second kind quietly produces nothing for an applicant who assumes the first.

Of the programs we have sourced so far, most consider every admitted student automatically. Yale SOM does not: "Scholarships are awarded post-admission through a short, supplemental application process. Only candidates who complete the application will be considered for grant aid." Kellogg is automatic except its Finance Fellows award, which needs a supplemental essay in the same round.

The rankings library carries this as a sortable column with a filter, each entry quoted from the school's own financial aid pages and dated.

External Fellowships Worth Knowing

These are awarded by outside organizations rather than by a school, and several are named repeatedly across the aid pages we sourced. Amounts and eligibility change every cycle, so treat the awarding body's own page as the authority and confirm before relying on any figure.

  • The Consortium for Graduate Study in Management. Named in the aid pages of Michigan Ross and Yale SOM among others; applicants apply through the Consortium's own process alongside member schools.
  • Forte Foundation Fellowships. Named by Berkeley Haas and Yale SOM; Yale states over $4M in Forte Fellowships went to SOM students in 2024.
  • Reaching Out MBA (ROMBA) Fellowship. Named by Berkeley Haas and Yale SOM.
  • Knight-Hennessy Scholars. Named on Stanford GSB's financial aid page; a separate application to Stanford's university-wide program.
  • Yellow Ribbon. Covered above; a VA and school partnership rather than an independent fellowship.

We list these because we found them cited on official school pages during sourcing. We are deliberately not publishing award amounts or deadlines for them until each is verified against the awarding organization's own site, which is work still outstanding.

What We Could Not Verify

  • Whether an MBA counts as a professional program under the new limits. Three primary sources refused this environment, as described above.
  • Award amounts and deadlines for the external fellowships listed above. Named only, with no figures, until sourced from each organization directly.
  • Participation rates at most schools. Harvard Business School publishes about 50 percent receiving need-based scholarship with 10 percent at full tuition; most programs publish no rate at all, and we show a dash rather than an estimate.
