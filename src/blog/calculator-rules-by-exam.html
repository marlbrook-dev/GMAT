Four of the five tests allow a calculator somewhere, and none allows one everywhere. The GMAT gives you an on-screen calculator in Data Insights but not in Quantitative Reasoning (GMAC, 2026), and the GRE gives you one in Quantitative Reasoning (ETS, 2026). The SAT and the ACT allow a calculator on math only, either Desmos built into the test or a handheld model of your own that meets their rules (College Board, 2026; ACT, 2026). The LSAT has no math and allows none (LSAC, 2026). Here is each rule, and what it means for how you practice.

GMAT: Data Insights Only

An on-screen calculator is available in the Data Insights section (GMAC, 2026). GMAC's guide to the section adds that, unlike the Quant section of the GMAT, you have access to an onscreen calculator there, and advises making sure you are not wasting time doing long calculations (GMAC, 2026). So the 21 Quantitative Reasoning questions are worked without one. You cannot bring a calculator of your own either: at a test center, calculators are among the items stored in a locker (mba.com, 2026). Our Data Insights guide covers when the calculator helps and when it slows you down.

GRE: On Screen in Quant

ETS lists an on-screen calculator for the Quantitative Reasoning section among the GRE's test design features, alongside the Mark and Review features and the ability to change answers within a section (ETS, 2026). It is part of the test itself, so the way to learn it is the way ETS suggests for all of those features: its free POWERPREP Online practice tests. Our GRE format guide covers the Quantitative Reasoning sections.

LSAT: No Calculator

The LSAT's three scored sections are two of Logical Reasoning and one of Reading Comprehension, and none of them is a math section (LSAC, 2026). LSAC's test day advice is to leave your watches, fitness tracking devices, calculators, tablets and any other electronic device in a different room, and it notes that the presence of a prohibited electronic device is the number one reason test sessions are cancelled (LSAC, 2026). Scratch paper is permitted for the multiple-choice sections, though not for LSAT Argumentative Writing.

SAT: Desmos or Your Own, on Math Only

Bluebook has a Desmos calculator built in, with graphing and scientific options you can switch between at any point in the Math section, and you may use your own approved handheld calculator instead (College Board, 2026). Calculators are not required, but College Board recommends bringing a familiar, approved model, and you are responsible for bringing one that works. You may not use a calculator on the Reading and Writing section, and it goes under your desk during those modules.

A handheld calculator has to meet College Board's rules. Only calculators without computer algebra system (CAS) functionality may be used, and College Board's prohibited list names CAS models such as the Casio ClassPad 300, 330 and 400. You must remove programs with algebra functionality and any stored documents, turn off all sound, and use only battery-operated equipment, with no power cords. Students cannot share calculators, and using one to share information or remove test content means dismissal and canceled scores. College Board no longer keeps a list of permitted graphing calculators, only the list of prohibited ones, and it does not recommend four-function calculators because they typically do not follow the order of operations (College Board, 2026).

ACT: Your Own, on Math Only

Calculators are allowed only on the mathematics test, and ACT says all of its problems may be solved without one. On paper you must bring your own permitted calculator, since testing staff do not provide them: four-function, scientific and graphing models are allowed unless prohibited. If you test online, the Desmos graphing calculator is built into the testing platform during the Math section (ACT, 2026).

Calculators with built-in or downloaded computer algebra system functionality are prohibited, including every Texas Instruments model number beginning with TI-89 or TI-92 and the TI-Nspire CAS; the TI-Nspire without CAS is permitted. Some calculators have to be modified first: remove all documents and any programs with CAS functionality, remove paper tape, turn off the sound, cover an infrared data port completely with heavy opaque material, and remove power cords. A talking or Braille calculator needs prior approval (ACT, 2026). Our ACT format guide covers the math test itself.

How to Practice for Each Rule

Practice the way you will test. For the GMAT, that means Quantitative Reasoning without a calculator, and Data Insights with the on-screen one used sparingly. For the GRE, it means the on-screen calculator in POWERPREP rather than a phone or a handheld. For the SAT and the ACT, choose between the built-in Desmos and a handheld model early, check the model against the prohibited list, and do your practice tests with it, so that test day brings no new buttons. For the LSAT, the only tools are the questions and your scratch paper.

Whichever test you are preparing for, the free trainers for the GMAT, GRE, LSAT, SAT and ACT run an adaptive round with no account needed, and report an estimated range from your practice, never a predicted score.
