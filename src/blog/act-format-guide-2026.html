The enhanced ACT in 2026 has three required sections: English, 50 questions in 35 minutes; math, 45 questions in 50 minutes; and reading, 36 questions in 40 minutes, for 131 questions in 125 minutes (ACT, 2026). Science, 40 questions in 40 minutes, and a 40-minute writing essay are optional. Each section is scored 1 to 36, the Composite averages English, math and reading only, and the test costs $70.

The pace differs more than the section lengths suggest. English gives you 42 seconds a question, math and reading about 67, and science 60. A term worth defining: pace budget, the time you can spend on each question before the section clock, rather than the content, decides your score. English has the tightest budget by a wide margin.

What Did the Enhanced ACT Change?

The enhanced ACT reached national online testing in April 2025 and paper testing on national Saturdays in September 2025, and it arrives on paper for State and District testing in spring 2026 (ACT, 2026). Three changes matter most:

  • Three required sections. English, math and reading are required; science and writing are optional add-ons.
  • Science left the Composite. The Composite is now the average of English, math and reading; science is optional and feeds a separate STEM score. Our guide to the ACT Composite works through the rounding.
  • Four answer choices everywhere. Every section now offers four choices, including math, which carried five on the ACT before the enhanced test.

How Is the ACT Delivered and Scored?

You can test on paper, online, or online on your own computer brought to a test site, and ACT offers the test seven times a year at test centers across the United States (ACT, 2026). Multiple-choice scores are reported in two to eight weeks, or five to eight weeks if you took the writing test. You can take the ACT up to 12 times, though ACT suggests most students retest only two to three times, and once you have tested more than once ACT calculates a superscore from your best section scores.

What Does Each Section Test?

ACT reports each section in categories with published weights (ACT, 2026). English reports Production of Writing (38 to 43 percent), Knowledge of Language (18 to 23 percent) and Conventions of Standard English (38 to 43 percent). Reading reports Key Ideas and Details (44 to 52 percent), Craft and Structure (26 to 33 percent) and Integration of Knowledge and Ideas (19 to 26 percent). Math puts 80 percent in Preparing for Higher Math and 20 percent in Integrating Essential Skills. Knowing the weights tells you where practice time pays: in English, conventions and production of writing together make up most of the section.
