A good ACT score is one above the national middle and inside the range your colleges publish, and ACT's own national ranks settle the first part: a Composite of 20 is at the 63rd percentile, 24 at the 80th, 28 at the 91st and 30 at the 94th (ACT, 2026). The average Composite is 19.1. These ranks apply to tests taken from September 2026 through August 2027 and compare you with recent ACT-tested graduates.

How Should You Read a National Rank?

A national rank is the percentage of recent ACT-tested graduates who scored at or below a given score. ACT reports it as your US Rank. So a rank of 89 at a Composite of 27 means 89 percent scored 27 or lower, which leaves about 11 percent at 28 or above. The mean Composite is 19.1 with a standard deviation of 6.1 (ACT, 2026), so the middle of the distribution sits near 19, and each point above it passes a large group of test takers until the mid 20s, where the steps in rank start to shrink. Why Does the Same Score Rank Differently by Section?

Each section has its own distribution, so an identical score earns a different rank. Reading has the highest section mean, 20.0, against 19.0 in math and 18.4 in English (ACT, 2026), and a reading score therefore ranks lower than the same number in math.

What ACT Score Should You Aim For?

Start from the colleges, not the national table. Look up the middle range each college on your list publishes for the students it admits, and aim for a Composite at or above the middle of the highest one. Then work backwards: the Composite averages English, math and reading, so our guide to the Composite shows how few section points separate one Composite from the next. If you test more than once, ACT calculates a superscore from your best section scores, and ACT says that on average it takes students two to three tries to reach their testing goals (ACT, 2026).
