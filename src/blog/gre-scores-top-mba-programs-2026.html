GRE scores for top MBA programs sit in a narrow band: at fifteen programs in our school library, the published class medians and averages run from 158 to 166 in Quantitative Reasoning and from 159 to 164 in Verbal Reasoning (school class profiles, 2025 and 2026). The same score means very different things in the two sections. A 164 in Quant is the 63rd percentile of GRE test takers; a 164 in Verbal is the 93rd (ETS, 2026).

Why Does the Same Score Mean More in Verbal?

ETS reports each section's percentile against everyone who took the GRE, and the two sections have different score distributions. A score that reads the same is a much higher percentile in Verbal than in Quant:

A 160 in Quant is the 50th percentile; a 160 in Verbal is ahead of 82 percent of test takers.

How Many MBA Students Take the GRE?

A few schools publish how many of their students submitted each test. 44 percent of Harvard Business School's Class of 2027 submitted a GRE, alongside 34 percent who submitted a GMAT Focus and 28 percent a Classic GMAT, with some submitting more than one (HBS, 2025). At Chicago Booth, 42 percent of the class submitted a GRE (Chicago Booth, 2025). GMAT vs GRE for MBA admissions covers how to choose between the two.

How Should You Read Your Own GRE Score Against These?

  • Look up each section's percentile, not the raw score: our GRE score calculator does it from ETS's table.
  • Compare a school's median with a median and its average with an average, and check which class the figure describes.
  • Read these as the middle of an admitted class, not a cutoff: every figure here is a median or an average, so admitted students scored on both sides of it.
