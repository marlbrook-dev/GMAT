Every test maker treats a missed test differently. GMAC marks a missed GMAT a No Show, keeps the fee and makes you wait 24 hours to book again (GMAC, 2026). ETS keeps the fee for a missed GRE but tells schools nothing (ETS, 2026). LSAC reports an LSAT absence to law schools unless you withdraw by the night before (LSAC, 2026). College Board offers a makeup SAT when a test center cannot open or the test is misadministered (College Board, 2026), and ACT lets you move a missed test to a later date for a change fee (ACT, 2026). Here is each rule, and what to do before test day if you already know you cannot make it.

GMAT: No Show, Lost Fee, 24-Hour Wait

If you miss your GMAT and do not meet GMAC's emergency criteria, you are marked as a No Show, your exam fee is forfeited, you must wait 24 hours before you can schedule a new appointment, and a new exam fee applies (GMAC, 2026). The emergency criteria are a serious illness or injury, active military orders, or the death of an immediate family member, each with supporting documentation. A verified emergency can bring a free reschedule or a full refund, reviewed case by case, and GMAC warns not to cancel the appointment yourself, as that may forfeit the refund (GMAC, 2026).

A missed appointment at least costs no attempt: appointments that are canceled before the exam or missed do not count toward the limit of five GMAT attempts in a rolling 12-month period (GMAC, 2026). If you know in advance, act more than 24 hours ahead: an appointment cannot be rescheduled or canceled within 24 hours of its start, and an exam not rescheduled or canceled before then forfeits the entire fee (mba.com, 2026). GRE: Fee Forfeited, Nothing Sent to Schools

If you are absent from a GRE administration, you forfeit your test fee, and institutions do not receive any information about your absence or about any previous scores you may have on file (ETS, 2026). Lateness counts too. Testing at home, you sign in at least 15 minutes before your scheduled time, and if you are more than 12 minutes late, your test is cancelled (ETS, 2026). At a test center, a late arrival may not be admitted, and the test fee would be forfeited (ETS, 2026).

The deadline that matters is four days before your test date, or 10 days in Mainland China. By then you can reschedule, for a fee, or cancel and get half of the original test fee back; a test not rescheduled by then forfeits its fee (ETS, 2026).

LSAT: Withdraw by the Night Before, or an Absence Is Reported

An LSAT you register for and miss does not simply disappear. LSAT results reported to the law schools you apply to include scores, cancellations and absences, from the current testing year and the prior five (LSAC, 2026). The way to keep an absence off your file is to withdraw: if you find after the refund deadline that you cannot take the LSAT, withdraw your registration by 11:59 p.m. ET the day before you are scheduled to test. Withdrawing prevents the absentee notation, but you do not receive a refund (LSAC, 2026).

Two more rules apply. If you do not schedule a testing time through Prometric by your administration's scheduling deadline, your registration is withdrawn automatically, without a refund (LSAC, 2026). And absences and withdrawals do not count against LSAC's limits on repeating the LSAT, while canceled scores do (LSAC, 2026). Moving to another test date is an option up to the day before testing, free through the registration deadline and $153 or $253 after it, as our guide to registration deadlines explains.

SAT: Makeup Tests for Closures and Misadministrations

College Board may offer a makeup SAT if your test center is unable to open on test day, or if a misadministration or irregularity occurs, and it contacts you if a makeup test is needed. At a makeup, you may take only the tests you registered for on the original date, at the center for which you are registered or authorized, and you must take the entire test (College Board, 2026).

A refund comes with canceling. Cancel by Thursday 11:59 p.m. ET before test day and you receive the registration fee back, less a cancellation fee: $34 before the change deadline, $44 after it. For an absence, the one refund College Board's refunds page names is for the score reports you ordered with registration, which are refundable only if you are absent on test day (College Board, 2026). To test on a different date, you cancel and register for a new test (College Board, 2026).

ACT: Move the Test to Another Date

ACT has the most forgiving rule. If you miss the test for any reason, you can request and pay for a test date change by the regular deadline for the new date. Registering for a new date instead forfeits the original date's basic fee, and if you do not test on another date, the registration fee and any additional fees are nonrefundable (ACT, 2026). The change fee is $49, and all ACT fees are nonrefundable unless noted (ACT, 2026).

Being late is the same as missing. Plan to be inside the test center no later than 8:00 a.m., because late arrivals cannot be admitted (ACT, 2026).

If You Know You Cannot Make It

Every one of these rules is kinder before test day than after it. Change a GMAT more than 24 hours ahead, a GRE four days ahead, and an SAT by the Thursday before; withdraw from the LSAT by the night before to keep an absence off the record your law schools see; and move an ACT to a date whose regular deadline has not passed. Our guide to canceling a score covers the other decision, when you do sit the test and wish you had not.

Whichever test you are preparing for, the free trainers for the GMAT, GRE, LSAT, SAT and ACT run an adaptive round with no account needed, and report an estimated range from your practice, never a predicted score.
