For MBA classes that entered in fall 2025, the Class of 2027, top US programs report GMAT Focus Edition central scores between 625 and 689: Stanford GSB averages 689, Harvard's median is 685, NYU Stern averages 682, and Yale SOM, Berkeley Haas, and MIT Sloan all sit at a 675 median, each per the school's own class profile (2025). The same profiles list Classic-edition figures near 700 to 740 for the shrinking group admitted on the old scale. The two sets of numbers describe the same classes on different scales, and reading them together is where applicants slip.

Why the Focus Column Looks Lower Than the Classic Column

It is the scale, not the students. The GMAT Focus Edition tops out at 805 and distributes scores differently than the Classic exam's 200 to 800 scale. On GMAC's official concordance tables, a Classic 700 corresponds to a Focus 655, and both sit at the 90.5th percentile (mba.com, 2026). So Yale's 675 Focus median is a higher-percentile result than a Classic 700, even though the digits read lower. Any time you compare across the two columns, translate to percentiles first; our walkthrough of the GMAT score chart shows how.

How to Set a Target Score From This Table

Pick the schools you actually intend to apply to, take the highest Focus figure among them, and treat the published middle 80 percent range, not the central number, as the field you are playing on. A score inside the range makes the GMAT a neutral fact in your file; chasing points above the median buys less than strengthening essays and recommendations once you are inside it.
