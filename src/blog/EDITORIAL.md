# The Study Room: editorial brief

Strategy source of truth: `design/guidelines/seo-content.md`. This file adds the house rules and the verified fact sheet writers must use. Generator: `src/build_blog.py` (validates on every build and fails loudly).

## Voice
Institutional, plain, even-handed. Confident but never salesy. Short sentences. No hype words (unlock, supercharge, game-changer). Never trash competitors or other tests. American English.

## House style (build fails on violation)
- No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere. Use commas, colons, periods, or the word "to" in ranges.
- GMAT Focus Edition total scores run 205 to 805 (ending in 5); sections score 60 to 90. Never present a Classic (200 to 800) number as a Focus number or vice versa. Always label which edition a number belongs to. Compare editions by percentile, not raw score.
- Titles 60 characters or fewer, keyword in the first half. Meta description 155 characters or fewer, keyword included.
- One H1 (the generator provides it from the title). Body uses h2/h3 only, question-phrased where natural. Answer the title's question in the first 80 words.
- At least one data table per post. Internal links to at least 2 sibling posts. FAQ block of 3 to 5 Q&As.
- Categories: Exam Guides, Study Science, Success Stories, Company News.

## Data rules (non-negotiable)
- Never state a school statistic, score median, percentile, or class-profile number from memory. Use ONLY the verified fact sheet below, with the source and year in parentheses right after the number.
- Approved sources: official school websites and class profiles, mba.com / GMAC, Poets & Quants, Financial Times, US News, Bloomberg Businessweek, Forbes, BusinessBecause, QS. Never cite or link GMAT Club, Quora, Wikipedia, GyanDhan, Pagalguy, or forums.
- Never invent statistics about Start From Nowhere: no user counts, no score-gain claims, no testimonials. The product facts below are the only product claims allowed.
- Success stories are reader-submitted only. Never fabricate one.
- If a fact is not on the sheet, write around it or state that the school publishes the figure in its class profile.

Digital SAT format and content (College Board, verified September 14, 2026):
- Total score 400 to 1600 in 10-point intervals; Reading and Writing and Math each scored 200 to 800 (College Board, SAT test structure, 2025).
- Reading and Writing: two 32-minute modules of 27 questions each, 54 questions in 64 minutes. Math: two 35-minute modules of 22 questions each, 44 questions in 70 minutes. 98 questions, 2 hours 14 minutes of testing, with a 10-minute break between sections (College Board, 2025).
- Section-adaptive, not question-adaptive: performance on the first module of a section determines whether the second module is the harder or the easier form. College Board does not publish the routing threshold, so never state one as official (College Board, 2025).
- Reading and Writing passages run 25 to 150 words, each followed by one multiple-choice question with four answer choices, drawn from literature, history and social studies, the humanities, and science (College Board, 2025).
- The four Reading and Writing content domains and their operational question ranges: Craft and Structure 13 to 15, Information and Ideas 12 to 14, Standard English Conventions 11 to 15, Expression of Ideas 8 to 12. Within a module the domains appear in that order, Craft and Structure first, and questions testing similar skills are grouped and run easiest to hardest (Digital SAT Suite Specifications Overview, Table 2).
- The four Math content domains and their ranges: Algebra 13 to 15, Advanced Math 13 to 15, Problem-Solving and Data Analysis 5 to 7, Geometry and Trigonometry 5 to 7. Math questions run easiest to hardest across each module (same document, Table 3).
- Bluebook has an embedded Desmos calculator, graphing or scientific, that students can toggle between at any point in the Math section, or they can use their own approved handheld calculator (College Board, calculator policy, 2026). Bluebook also has an annotation tool, lets students cross out an answer option they think is wrong, and lets them mark questions for review (College Board, What to Expect on Test Day, 2026).
- Fully digital in the US since March 2024, taken in the Bluebook app (College Board Newsroom, 2024). SAT registration fee $68, with additional fees listed through December 2026; students testing in another country are sent to a separate international fee schedule (College Board, 2026). Most scores from weekend tests are released 2 to 4 weeks after test day (College Board, 2026).
- Class of 2025 results (College Board, 2025 SAT Suite Annual Report, Total Group; each student counted once at their most recent score): 2,004,965 test takers; mean total 1029 (SD 235), Reading and Writing 521 (SD 121), Math 508 (SD 126). Total score bands: 1400 to 1600, 149,767; 1200 to 1390, 357,574; 1000 to 1190, 554,819; 800 to 990, 609,159; 600 to 790, 281,174; 400 to 590, 52,472. So 7.5 percent scored 1400 or higher and 25.3 percent 1200 or higher (our arithmetic on the counts). Score reports show an All Tester Percentile, the percent of the past three cohorts of 12th-grade SAT takers worldwide at or below a score (College Board, Understanding Scores, fall 2026).
- Students can take the SAT as many times as they want; College Board recommends at least twice, in the spring of junior year and the fall of senior year (College Board, 2026). Approved SAT sources: College Board and its SAT Suite site, and the publishers already approved above. ACT format and scoring (ACT, verified September 26, 2026; every figure is mirrored with its URL in data/exams.json):
- English 50 questions in 35 minutes, math 45 in 50, reading 36 in 40: 131 questions in 125 minutes. Optional science is 40 questions in 40 minutes; with it the timed multiple choice runs 171 questions in 2 hours 45 minutes. The optional writing essay takes 40 minutes after a 5-minute break (ACT, 2026).
- Each section is scored 1 to 36. The Composite is the average of English, math and reading, rounded to the nearest whole number: fractions less than one-half round down, one-half or more round up. Science left the Composite for national online testing in April 2025 and for all ACT tests in September 2025; it still feeds a STEM score, the average of math and science, and an ELA score averages English, reading and writing when writing is taken. Writing is scored 2 to 12 (ACT, 2026).
- The superscore averages your best English, math and reading scores across attempts and is rounded the same way. It is calculated automatically once you have tested more than once since September 2016, it leaves out science and writing, and ACT advises checking each school's own score policy (ACT superscore FAQ, 2026).
- The ACT test is $70; the science add-on $5 and the writing add-on $25, or $100 for all three; late registration $42, standby testing $75, each additional score report $20 (ACT, 2026).
- Four answer choices in every section, including math, which had five before the enhanced test. Paper, online at a test center, or on your own device, with seven national Saturday dates a year. No limit on attempts: ACT says there is none, and that students take the test 2 to 3 times on average to reach their goals (ACT, read September 27, 2026; the page said 12 attempts before, INC-0172). Multiple-choice scores in two to eight weeks, five to eight with writing (ACT, 2026).
- National ranks, the percent of recent ACT-tested graduates at or below each score, for tests taken September 2026 through August 2027 (graduates of 2024, 2025 and 2026). Score scales (each test maker's own pages, read September 27, 2026; the scales are mirrored with their URLs in data/exams.json, and the percentiles in data/*_percentiles.json, all read weekly):
- GMAT (GMAC, GMAT Exam Scores): the Focus Edition total runs 205 to 805, from three sections each 60 to 90 and equally weighted; each section score rests on the number of questions answered, whether the answers are correct, and the difficulty and other parameters of the questions answered. GMAC's percentile ranking is the percentage of test takers a candidate performed better than; its Focus table (509,182 test takers, 2021 to 2026) puts 805 at 100, 705 at 98, 655 at 90, 605 at 69 and 555 at 47. GMAC also says the GMAT has not been equated with other tests, that comparing it with other tests such as the GRE is not appropriate, and that GMAT scores cannot be estimated from other tests.
- GRE (ETS, GRE Scoring): three scores and no total: Verbal Reasoning and Quantitative Reasoning 130 to 170 in 1-point increments, Analytical Writing 0 to 6 in half-point increments; a section with no questions answered is reported as No Score. Percentiles are in the GRE percentiles block above.
- LSAT (LSAC, LSAT Scoring): the raw score is the number of questions answered correctly, every question weighted the same, no deduction for incorrect answers, converted to a 120 to 180 scale; no section scores. Percentiles are in the LSAT percentiles block above.
- SAT (College Board, Understanding Scores and SAT Scores): the total is the sum of Reading and Writing and Math, each 200 to 800, so 400 to 1600; the report adds a score range and percentiles. PSAT/NMSQT and PSAT 10 run 320 to 1520, PSAT 8/9 240 to 1440, so a PSAT score is not an SAT score.
- ACT (ACT, Understanding Your Scores): each section 1 to 36; the raw score is the number correct with no penalty for incorrect answers; the Composite averages English, math and reading, rounded to the nearest whole number; optional writing 2 to 12, the sum of two domain scores; STEM is the average of math and science, ELA of English, reading and writing if taken. ACT and College Board publish official ACT and SAT concordance tables, which ACT calls estimates, not exact conversions (an ACT 30 is roughly an SAT 1370).

- ACT (ACT, Test Day): inside the test center no later than 8:00 a.m.; late arrivals cannot be admitted. Bring: admission ticket (printed from MyACT after uploading a photo), photo ID, sharpened No. 2 pencils with erasers (no pens or mechanical pencils), a charged laptop and charger for online testing on your own device. Optional: a watch with no alarm (stays on the desk), a permitted calculator for math, an ACT-approved bilingual dictionary (inspected), snacks during breaks outside the testing room. Not allowed: textbooks, notes, scratch paper, highlighters, colored pens, correction fluid, reading material, tobacco; phones, smart watches, fitness bands and Wi-Fi or recording devices off and out of sight, never used in the room or during breaks.

- GMAT (mba.com, Sending Your Score; GMAC help center, GMAT Superscore articles): you choose whether to send each score, and programs have no access to your official scores unless you send them; since August 12, 2026 a report carries the GMAT Superscore when one exists, with the section scores behind it and the date and delivery method of each, and it cannot be left off (see the Superscore note above). - ACT (ACT, Sending Your ACT Scores): scores go automatically to the high school and colleges selected at registration; afterwards you choose what to send at any time, the scores from an individual test event, your Superscore, or both; the Superscore is calculated automatically after more than one test since September 2016 and averages the highest section scores across all test dates; colleges can receive test events from September 2011 on; $20 per test date, per report, and a $32 archive fee for scores 3 or more years old.

- ACT (ACT, Test Dates and Deadlines; Fees): a registration deadline, a late deadline and a Photo Upload & Standby Deadline for each national date; deadlines at 11:59 pm Central Time; late fee $42 on a registration or test date change made in the late period; standby testing $75, paper testing only.

Missing a test (each test maker's own pages, read September 28, 2026; five of these rules join data/exams.json, where the source check reads them weekly, beside the fee and date change rules already there):
- GMAT (GMAC help center, How Can I Cancel/Reschedule My Appointment If I Have An Emergency or Missed My Appointment; Retake Policies and Attempt Limits; mba.com, Exam Payment): a missed exam outside the emergency criteria is marked a No Show, the exam fee is forfeited, a new appointment can be scheduled only after 24 hours, and a new exam fee applies. Emergencies (serious illness or injury, active military orders, death of an immediate family member), with documentation, may bring a free reschedule or a full refund, case by case; do not cancel the appointment yourself first. Missed appointments do not count toward the five attempts in a rolling 12 months. Nothing can be changed within 24 hours of the appointment.
- GRE (GRE Information Bulletin 2026-27; ETS, Test Center Test Day): absence forfeits the test fee, and institutions receive no information about the absence or about earlier scores; at home, sign in at least 15 minutes early, and more than 12 minutes late cancels the test; at a test center, a late arrival may not be admitted and forfeits the fee. Reschedule or cancel (half the fee back) no later than four days before, 10 in Mainland China.
- LSAT (LSAC, LSAT Scoring; JD Refunds): results reported to law schools include scores, cancellations and absences; after the refund deadline, withdrawing by 11:59 p.m. ET the day before the scheduled test prevents the absentee notation but brings no refund; a registration with no testing time scheduled through Prometric by the scheduling deadline is withdrawn automatically, without a refund. Absences and withdrawals do not count against the limits on repeating the LSAT.
- SAT (College Board, Makeup Tests; Refunds; Canceling a Registration): a makeup test may be offered when a test center is unable to open or a misadministration or irregularity occurs, and College Board makes contact; only the tests registered for, at the registered or authorized center, and the entire test. Canceling by Thursday 11:59 p.m. ET before test day refunds the registration fee less $34 (before the change deadline) or $44 (after it); score reports ordered with registration are refundable only if absent on test day.
- ACT (ACT, Registration; Fees; Test Day): a missed test for any reason can be moved with a test date change requested and paid for by the new date's regular deadline; registering for a new date instead forfeits the original basic fee; the change fee is $49 and fees are nonrefundable unless noted. Inside the test center by 8:00 a.m.; late arrivals cannot be admitted.

What a score report shows (each test maker's own pages, read September 28, 2026; seven of these rules join data/exams.json, where the source check reads them weekly):
- GMAT (mba.com, Official Score Reports; GMAC help center, Who Has Access to My Score, and What Information Do They Receive?; Why Did My Score (Percentile) Change?; What If a School Has Asked Me to Upload a PDF of My Score Report?): total and section scores, percentile rankings, insights by section, question type and content domain, and time management details; the GMAT Superscore where applicable. A program receives profile information, the scores and any self-reported information (undergraduate GPA, intended area of study); a 10th Edition report also carries the AWA essay. No physical or PDF version, and a screenshot is not an official score report. Percentile tables are updated annually and the report updates with them; the total score does not change.
- GRE (ETS, Getting Your GRE General Test Scores): the Test-taker Score Report holds contact information, date of birth, gender, intended graduate major, test dates, scores with percentile ranks, recipients and a 5-year record of scores reported; the Institution Score Report holds contact information, date of birth, gender, intended major and the test dates and scores chosen with ScoreSelect, with percentile ranks, and no other recipients, unreported scores or sign of other GRE tests. Photos and essay responses of each reported administration are in the ETS Data Manager for recipients. No Score (NS) for a measure with no answers.
- LSAT (LSAC, LSAT Scoring): current score, all reportable results up to 12 with absences and cancellations, a percentile rank for each score (percentage of test takers scoring lower over the previous three testing years, updated by the end of July each year), and the score band.
- SAT (College Board, What Do My Scores Mean?): total score and the two section scores, a Score Range (how much scores would likely vary on a different administration under identical conditions), percentiles, and Knowledge and Skills for the eight content areas, four per section; a Career Insights Snapshot for US students, examples rather than recommendations.
- ACT (ACT, Understanding Your ACT Scores): Composite and section scores 1 to 36, writing 2 to 12 if taken, STEM (math and science) and ELA (English, reading and writing if taken) scores, and reporting categories with the number of questions, the number correct and the percentage correct. ACT's page gives the writing score two different ways (a sum of two domain scores, and a rounded average of four), so do not describe how it is computed.

The math on each test (each test maker's own pages, read September 28, 2026; four of these rules join data/exams.json, where the source check reads them weekly):
- GMAT (GMAC, GMAT Focus Edition exam and scores page): Quantitative Reasoning is 21 multiple-choice Problem Solving questions in 45 minutes, measuring algebraic and arithmetic foundational knowledge and how it is applied to solve problems; no calculator there, an on-screen one in Data Insights, whose 20 questions in 45 minutes are data sufficiency, multi-source reasoning, table analysis, graphics interpretation and two-part analysis.
- GRE (ETS, Overview of the Quantitative Reasoning Measure): four content areas, arithmetic, algebra, geometry and data analysis, with ETS's own topic lists; high school mathematics and statistics generally no higher than a second course in algebra, with no trigonometry, calculus or other higher-level mathematics; inferential statistics and constructing proofs are not tested; many questions are word problems. An on-screen calculator in Quantitative Reasoning.
- SAT (College Board, Digital SAT Suite of Assessments Specifications Overview; How the SAT Is Structured): Math is 44 questions in two 35-minute modules; Algebra and Advanced Math about 35% each (13 to 15 questions), Problem-Solving and Data Analysis and Geometry and Trigonometry about 15% each (5 to 7 questions); most questions multiple choice, some student-produced responses; Desmos built into Bluebook or an approved handheld.
- ACT (ACT, Understanding Your ACT Scores; Preparing for the ACT): mathematics is 45 questions in 50 minutes; Preparing for Higher Math 80% (Number and Quantity 10 to 12%, Algebra, Functions and Geometry 17 to 20% each, Statistics and Probability 12 to 15%), Integrating Essential Skills 20%, and Modeling scored across the other categories.
- The LSAT has no math section: two scored Logical Reasoning sections, one scored Reading Comprehension section and one unscored section of either type.

The verbal side of each test (each test maker's own pages, read September 28, 2026; three of these rules join data/exams.json, where the source check reads them weekly):
- GMAT (GMAC, GMAT Focus Edition exam and scores page): Verbal Reasoning is 23 multiple-choice Reading Comprehension and Critical Reasoning questions in 45 minutes, measuring the ability to read and comprehend written material and to reason and evaluate arguments; Reading Comprehension measures understanding words and statements, logical relationships between significant points, drawing inferences and following the development of quantitative concepts; Critical Reasoning measures making and evaluating arguments and formulating or evaluating a plan of action.
- GRE (ETS, Overview of the Verbal Reasoning Measure; Test Structure): two sections of 12 and 15 questions in 18 and 23 minutes; about half the measure is passages with questions and half is reading, interpreting and completing sentences, groups of sentences or paragraphs; three question types, Reading Comprehension, Text Completion and Sentence Equivalence.
- LSAT (LSAC, FAQ; Test Format; Reading Comprehension): two scored Logical Reasoning sections, one scored Reading Comprehension section and one unscored section of either type, 35 minutes each; the unscored section can occur at any point; a Reading Comprehension section is four sets, each a selection followed by five to eight questions.
- SAT (College Board, Specifications Overview; How the SAT Is Structured): Reading and Writing is 54 questions in two 32-minute modules; Craft and Structure about 28% (13 to 15 questions), Information and Ideas about 26% (12 to 14), Standard English Conventions about 26% (11 to 15), Expression of Ideas about 20% (8 to 12); each module runs Craft and Structure, then Information and Ideas, Standard English Conventions and Expression of Ideas, with similar questions grouped and ordered from easiest to hardest.
- ACT (ACT, Preparing for the ACT): English 50 questions in 35 minutes, reported as Production of Writing (38 to 43 percent), Knowledge of Language (18 to 23 percent) and Conventions of Standard English (38 to 43 percent); reading 36 questions in 40 minutes, reported as Key Ideas and Details (44 to 52 percent), Craft and Structure (26 to 33 percent) and Integration of Knowledge and Ideas (19 to 26 percent).

The essay on each test (each test maker's own pages, read September 28, 2026; three of these rules join data/exams.json, where the source check reads them weekly):
- GMAT (GMAC, GMAT Focus Edition overview; mba.com support): three 45-minute sections, 64 questions in 2 hours 15 minutes, with no writing assessment; a report of a GMAT Exam, 10th Edition score includes a copy of its Analytical Writing Assessment essay.
- GRE (ETS, Test Structure; Scoring; Get Your Scores; Fees): Analytical Writing is one Analyze an Issue task in 30 minutes and always comes first; it is scored 0 to 6 in half-point increments; essay responses are available to score recipients in the ETS Data Manager; a score review of the Analytical Writing measure costs $60.
- LSAT (LSAC, Specifications of the LSAT and LSAT Argumentative Writing; LSAT Scoring): Argumentative Writing is an unscored writing sample, administered separately and only online through remote-proctoring software on the candidate's own computer; 15 minutes of prewriting analysis, which can be skipped after five, then 35 minutes to write; scores are released only with an approved writing sample on file, which can take up to three weeks to process, and the law school report includes the writing sample.
- SAT (College Board, What Is the SAT Essay?, dated February 28, 2024): the optional weekend SAT Essay was discontinued in 2021; the Essay section is only available in certain states where it is required for SAT School Day, comes after Reading and Writing and Math and adds 50 minutes; it asks for an analysis of how the author of a 650 to 750 word passage builds an argument, and its score is reported with that day's section scores.
- ACT (ACT, Test Day; Understanding Your Scores; Online Testing): the writing test is optional and adds 40 minutes, with five minutes to prepare before it; it is scored 2 to 12 across four domains, Ideas and Analysis, Development and Support, Organization, and Language Use and Conventions; it counts toward the ELA score but not the Composite or the superscore; scores with writing come in five to eight weeks. ACT's page describes how the overall writing score is formed in two different ways, so do not state how it is computed.
Guessing on each test (each test maker's own pages, read September 29, 2026; six of these rules join data/exams.json, where the source check reads them weekly):
- GMAT (GMAC, Exam Scores; mba.com, Exam Structure; mba.com help center): each section score is based on three factors, the number of questions answered, whether the answers are correct or incorrect, and the difficulty and other parameters of the questions answered; a response counts only after Next and then Yes are clicked, and one not confirmed before time runs out is not saved or scored; questions can be bookmarked, and after every question in a section is answered, up to three answers can be changed on the Question Review and Edit screen, which is skipped if no time remains.
- GRE (ETS, Test Structure): the scoring for Verbal Reasoning and Quantitative Reasoning takes into consideration the total number of questions answered correctly across the two sections and the difficulty level of the sections; within a section you can skip questions, go back and change answers, and use Mark and Review.
- LSAT (LSAC, LSAT Scoring): the score is based on the number of questions answered correctly, all weighted exactly the same, and there is no deduction for incorrect answers.
- SAT (College Board, SAT Understanding Scores for fall 2026; What to Expect): scores depend on whether answers are right or wrong, the difficulty of the questions and the probability that you might be guessing, and for most students it is better to guess than leave a question blank, especially after eliminating one or two wrong answers; within a module you can move back and forth and mark questions for review, but once you move on from a module you cannot go back to it.
- ACT (ACT, Understanding Your Scores; Preparing for the ACT Test, 2026 to 2027): scores are based on the number of questions answered correctly, with no penalty for incorrect answers; ACT advises answering every question and, when stuck, guessing, flagging the question and returning to it if there is time.
Do not say that any of the five takes points off for a wrong answer, and do not say the GMAT or the SAT counts only correct answers: GMAC counts the number answered, and College Board's scoring considers the probability of guessing.

How the adaptive tests adapt (each test maker's own pages, read September 29, 2026; three of these rules join data/exams.json, where the source check reads them weekly):
- GMAT (GMAC help center, What Does Computer-Adaptive Mean on the GMAT?; GMAC, Exam Scores): question by question; the exam typically starts with a medium-difficulty question, the next may be more difficult after a right answer and easier after a wrong one, and the score is based on the overall pattern of responses; each section score rests on the number of questions answered, whether the answers are correct, and the difficulty and other parameters of the questions; GMAC says trying to game the difficulty level will not improve your score.
- GRE (ETS, Test Structure): section by section; the first section of each measure is of average difficulty and the second section's difficulty depends on overall performance on the first; scoring considers the number answered correctly across the two sections and the difficulty of the sections; you can move forward and backward throughout a section.
- SAT (College Board, SAT structure; What to Expect; SAT Understanding Scores for fall 2026): module by module; each section has two equal-length modules, the first a broad mix of easy, medium and hard questions, the second more or less difficult depending on the first; Reading and Writing is 54 questions in two 32-minute modules and Math 44 in two 35-minute modules; you cannot go back to a module once you leave it.
LSAC's and ACT's pages on their test formats do not describe either test as adaptive; leave both out of any claim about adaptive testing rather than saying they are not adaptive.

When something goes wrong on test day (each test maker's own pages, read September 29, 2026; six of these rules join data/exams.json, where the source check reads them weekly):
- GMAT (GMAC, GMAT Policies and Procedures, last updated August 12, 2026): online test takers must notify the proctor of any technical issues immediately, one of the user agreements for the online exam; a test center disruption that you believe affected your performance is reported in person at the test center or to Customer Care within seven days of the exam date, and online technical concerns go to Customer Care within seven days; a score canceled for a Testing Issue (an event affecting the administration of an exam, such as an administrative or technical error) is noted on the Official Score Report as a T, schools may be notified if it was already reported, and GMAC may offer, in its sole discretion, a retest at no additional charge, a retest for the test fee, or a refund; a retest is the entire exam, and GMAC may require a test center; the canceled session still counts toward five attempts in a rolling 12-month period.
- GRE (ETS, GRE Information Bulletin 2026-27): if a technical problem at the test center makes it necessary to cancel the session, or scores could not be reported, a free new appointment or a full refund of the original fee; reimbursement of reasonable, documented travel expenses may be sought from GRE Services within 30 days of the original appointment; weather or other circumstances can delay a start or reschedule a test. The bulletin states this remedy for the test center; do not extend it to testing at home without a source that says so.
- LSAT (LSAC, frequently asked questions about the LSAT): testing remotely, in most cases the proctoring system saves progress and pauses the test while a technical issue is resolved, and you complete the security check-in again before resuming; the proctor decides whether you may resume more than once; if the connection cannot be restored during the scheduled window, the session is terminated and you are moved to a future registration date.
- SAT (College Board, What to Expect; Makeup Tests): if you have a problem during the test, raise your hand to ask the proctor for help or click the Help icon for troubleshooting tips; College Board may offer a makeup test when a test center cannot open or a misadministration or irregularity occurs, and contacts you if one is needed.
- ACT (ACT, Terms and Conditions): after a mistiming, technical difficulties, disruptions at the test location or other events that cancel or interrupt testing, ACT may decide that no further action is necessary, and that decision is final; otherwise, in its sole discretion, it corrects the error, cancels scores, or discontinues testing and offers a retest at no additional fee, normally on a future test date, or a refund. A test paid for by a state or district may be canceled without an option to retest.
