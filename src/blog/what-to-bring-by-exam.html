Every test center checks a photo ID, and none lets you use a phone during the test. Past that, the lists differ more than you might expect. At a GRE test center the only personal items allowed in the testing room are your ID and a mask (ETS, 2026), while the SAT has you bring your own testing device and pencils or pens, with a drink or snacks for the break (College Board, 2026). The GMAT allows no food or drink unless pre-authorized as an accommodation (mba.com, 2026), the LSAT puts phones, food and beverages in lockers (LSAC, 2026), and the ACT wants No. 2 pencils, not pens or mechanical pencils (ACT, 2026). Here is each test maker's list, and when to arrive.

GMAT: Very Little Goes In

GMAC's pre-test checklist asks you to confirm your appointment date, time and location, review the exam policies, plan your route with time for traffic, and arrive at least 30 minutes before your appointment to allow for check-in, with a valid photo ID whose name exactly matches your registration. You can bring into the test center your GMAT-approved identification, the appointment confirmation email from Pearson VUE, prescription eyeglasses without the case (tinted lenses are fine, smart glasses are not), a light sweater or light non-outerwear jacket, and permitted comfort items, which the test administrator may inspect (mba.com, 2026).

Almost everything else goes in a locker, from watches, cell phones and calculators to scratch paper, ear plugs, lip balm and dictionaries. Food, drink and candy are not allowed unless expressly pre-authorized as an accommodation, and weapons are banned from the whole test center, waiting areas included. At check-in you present your ID, provide a palm vein scan where the law permits one, sign the Test Taker Rules and Agreement digitally, and sit for a photograph (mba.com, 2026). GRE: Your ID, and a Mask If You Want One

ETS keeps the testing room almost bare. The only personal items you may bring into it are your ID and your mask, if you choose to wear one; test center staff tell you where to store everything else before the test. If you have been approved for accommodations for a health-related need, you may bring the equipment, beverages or snacks it covers (ETS, 2026), and our guide to accommodations explains how to ask.

Arrive at least 30 minutes before your test time to allow for check-in; if you arrive late, you may not be admitted, and your test fee is forfeited. Check-in is thorough. Your photo is taken and your ID verified, and you may be asked to empty your pockets, raise your pant legs above your ankles and your sleeves above your wrists, give a voice sample, be scanned with a metal detector wand, or remove your eyeglasses for inspection. You can ask for inspections by a staff member of the same gender or out of view of other people, though ETS cannot guarantee every center has the staff and space. You also sign a confidentiality statement, and if you do not sign it, you cannot test (ETS, 2026).

LSAT: Memorize Your LawHub Login

LSAC's checklist for test center candidates is short. Arrive up to 30 minutes before your scheduled time, with a valid photo ID, and have your LawHub username and password memorized, since they are the credentials you used to register and your start time is delayed if you do not know them. Lockers are provided, and all cell phones, food and beverages are stored in them while you take the test. After check-in you read and agree to the Certifying Statement and click Begin, and the time starts (LSAC, 2026).

The 10-minute intermission after the second section has rules of its own: no electronic devices, no discussing the test and no working on your scratch paper, and you check back in with staff, showing your ID, before the 10 minutes are over (LSAC, 2026). Our guide to breaks covers every test's.

SAT: Your Device, Charged and Set Up

College Board's list begins with your fully charged testing device, with the Bluebook application installed and exam setup completed. Bring your up-to-date admission ticket from Bluebook, preferably printed, which you can get after exam setup from 5 days before the test; an acceptable photo ID, which must be a physical document, not electronic; pencils or pens for scratch work; your College Board username and password; and an acceptable calculator for the Math section if you want one, though Bluebook has a calculator built in. Epinephrine auto-injectors are permitted without an accommodation, in a clear bag under your desk (College Board, 2026).

College Board also lists what is nice to have: a watch without an audible alarm, a charging cable or portable charger, a bag or backpack, a drink or snacks for your break, and a backup testing device (College Board, 2026). Your phone is another matter: staff collect and hold phones, or have you put them in a backpack away from your desk, and a device that makes noise or is used at any time, breaks included, gets you dismissed. In College Board's sample schedule, test center doors open at 7:45 a.m. and close at 8 a.m.; the time on your admission ticket is the one that counts (College Board, 2026).

ACT: No. 2 Pencils, and Be Inside by 8:00

Plan to be inside the test center no later than 8:00 a.m. to allow time for check-in, because late arrivals cannot be admitted. Bring your admission ticket, printed from MyACT after you upload your photo; an acceptable photo ID; and sharpened No. 2 pencils with erasers, since pens and mechanical pencils are not allowed. If you registered for online testing with your own device, bring a fully charged laptop and its charger. You may also bring a watch with no alarm, which stays on your desk, a permitted calculator for the math test, an ACT-approved bilingual dictionary, which staff will inspect, and snacks, which you eat during breaks outside the testing room (ACT, 2026).

Leave at home textbooks, notes, scratch paper, highlighters, colored pens, correction fluid, reading material and tobacco. Phones, smart watches, fitness bands and other devices with Wi-Fi or recording must be off and out of sight, and you may not use or access them in the testing room or during breaks; a device that is accessed or activated means dismissal, a test that is not scored, and possibly a confiscated device (ACT, 2026). Pack the Night Before

The one item every test center checks is your ID, and its name has to match your registration, so put it out the night before, along with the admission ticket the SAT and the ACT ask for. Then work back from your arrival time: at least 30 minutes early for the GMAT and the GRE, up to 30 minutes early for the LSAT, and by 8:00 a.m. for the ACT, or the time on your SAT admission ticket (mba.com, 2026; ETS, 2026; LSAC, 2026; ACT, 2026; College Board, 2026). Testing at home instead has rules of its own, which our guide to testing at home covers.

Whichever test you are preparing for, the free trainers for the GMAT, GRE, LSAT, SAT and ACT run an adaptive round with no account needed, and report an estimated range from your practice, never a predicted score.
