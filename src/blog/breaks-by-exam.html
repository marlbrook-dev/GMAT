Four of the five tests build in a break, and the GRE does not. The GMAT gives one optional 10-minute break, after the first or second section (mba.com, 2026). The LSAT has a 10-minute intermission after Section 2 (LSAC, 2026), the SAT one 10-minute break between its two sections (College Board, 2026), and the ACT a short break after its second test (ACT, 2026). The GRE General Test has no scheduled breaks (ETS, 2026). Here is when each break comes, and what you can do in it.

GMAT: One Optional Break, When You Choose

The GMAT is 2 hours and 15 minutes long, with one optional 10-minute break, and you decide when to take it: after the first section or after the second. Since you also choose the order of the three sections, you can put the break where it helps you most, for example before the section you find hardest (mba.com, 2026). One detail catches people out: if a section's time runs out, you skip that section's Question Review & Edit screen and move straight on to the optional break screen, or to the next section if you have already taken your break (mba.com, 2026).

GRE: No Scheduled Break

The shorter GRE General Test runs about 1 hour and 58 minutes in five sections, and ETS says it does not include any scheduled breaks (ETS, 2026). That is a change: before September 22, 2023, the test took about 3 hours and 45 minutes and had a 10-minute break after the third section (ETS, 2026). Our guide to how long each test is sets the five side by side.

At home, unscheduled breaks are not permitted at all. At a test center, taking repeated or lengthy unscheduled breaks, unless they are approved as an accommodation, is among the actions for which you can be dismissed and your scores canceled; you cannot reach your personal items during an unscheduled break except food, beverages or medication, and staff may inspect you again when you return. If a health-related need calls for extra or extended breaks, request them through ETS's accommodations procedures before you test (ETS, 2026).

LSAT: A 10-Minute Intermission

The LSAT has four multiple-choice sections, three scored and one unscored, and a 10-minute intermission between the second and third. You must tell your proctor the intermission has begun and wait for acknowledgement before leaving the testing area, and you must be back within the 10 minutes; an onscreen timer counts it down, and Section 3 does not begin until the full 10 minutes have passed. Every test taker with the standard intermission has to take all of it (LSAC, 2026).

The test is still in session. You can use the restroom, eat or drink something and stretch, but you may not use a phone or any other electronic device without an approved accommodation, use your computer for anything but the test, work on your scratch paper or discuss the test. The camera stays on. Afterwards the proctor checks your testing area again, and you confirm you followed the rules before Section 3 starts (LSAC, 2026).

SAT: One Break Between Sections

The SAT gives one 10-minute break, between the Reading and Writing section and the Math section. Because each student is timed individually in Bluebook, you take your break at a slightly different time from the people around you. You can take an unscheduled break if you need one, but you lose testing time. During breaks, leave your device open, keep your ID with you, since it is checked every time you enter the room, and leave any calculator on your desk (College Board, 2026). Our guide to test day ID covers what that ID has to be.

Phones stay out of reach the whole time: staff collect and hold them, or have you put them in a backpack away from your desk, including during breaks. If a prohibited device makes a noise or you try to use it at any time, breaks included, you are dismissed and your scores can be canceled (College Board, 2026).

ACT: A Short Break After the Second Test

ACT schedules a short break after the second test, and if you take the writing test, you get five minutes to relax and prepare before the essay. You may not leave the supervised test area or site, not even to go to your car, and electronic devices are not allowed during breaks: a device accessed or activated means dismissal and a test that is not scored. Snacks are allowed during breaks, outside the testing room (ACT, 2026).

Practice With the Breaks You Will Get

A practice test should match the real one's shape. For the GRE, that means about two hours at a stretch with no pause. For the GMAT, decide in practice where your break works best, since it is yours to place. For the LSAT, the SAT and the ACT, the break comes at a fixed point, so plan a snack and a drink for it where the rules allow one, and leave your phone out of the plan: the LSAT, the SAT and the ACT keep devices off limits during the break, and ETS says you may not access your phone during the GRE or during breaks (LSAC, 2026; College Board, 2026; ACT, 2026; ETS, 2026).

Whichever test you are preparing for, the free trainers for the GMAT, GRE, LSAT, SAT and ACT run an adaptive round with no account needed, and report an estimated range from your practice, never a predicted score.
