A good SAT score sits above the 50th percentile and inside the range your colleges publish. College Board's figures for the class of 2025 set the scale: the average total score was 1029, about a quarter of the class scored 1200 or higher, and 7.5 percent scored 1400 or more (College Board, 2025). Those figures count each of the 2,004,965 students who took the SAT in high school once, at their most recent score.

What Share Scored Above a Given Score?

Adding the bands from the top gives the share of the class at or above each threshold: 7.5 percent at 1400 or higher, 25.3 percent at 1200 or higher, 53.0 percent at 1000 or higher and 83.4 percent at 800 or higher. The average was 1029 with a standard deviation of 235, so a score one standard deviation above the mean is a little over 1260 (College Board, 2025).

What Does the Percentile on a Score Report Mean?

Your score report shows an All Tester Percentile. A score in the 70th percentile means 70 percent of the comparison group scored at or below it, and the group is the past three cohorts of students who took the SAT in 12th grade, for tests completed anywhere in the world (College Board, Understanding Scores, fall 2026). Because it pools three cohorts and counts at or below, it will not match the single-class shares above exactly.
