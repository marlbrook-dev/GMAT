Each of the five tests keeps its own scale. The GMAT Focus Edition runs from 205 to 805 (GMAC, 2026), the GRE reports Verbal and Quantitative scores of 130 to 170 each with no total (ETS, 2026), the LSAT runs from 120 to 180 (LSAC, 2026), the SAT from 400 to 1600 (College Board, 2026), and the ACT Composite from 1 to 36 (ACT, 2026). The numbers do not carry from one test to another: ACT and College Board publish an official concordance between the ACT and the SAT, and GMAC says GMAT scores should not be compared with other tests' at all. Here is how each scale is built, what its top looks like, and how to compare scores that sit on different ones.

GMAT: 205 to 805

The GMAT Focus Edition total runs from 205 to 805, built from three sections, Quantitative Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning and Data Insights, each scored 60 to 90 and each weighted equally toward the total (GMAC, 2026). GMAC says each section score rests on three things: the number of questions answered, whether the answers are correct or incorrect, and the difficulty and other parameters of the questions answered.

The earlier GMAT, the 10th Edition, ran from 200 to 800, and GMAC is plain that the two cannot be compared by their numbers: the scores are not on a common scale, and while scores of 600 and 605 may look similar, they represent very different performance levels on different skills (GMAC, 2026). The link between the two is percentile. GMAC's percentile ranking is the percentage of test takers a candidate performed better than, and in its Focus Edition table, from 509,182 test takers over 2021 to 2026, 805 is at 100 percent, 705 at 98, 655 at 90, 605 at 69 and 555 at 47 (GMAC, 2026). GRE: Three Scores, No Total

The GRE reports three scores: Verbal Reasoning, 130 to 170 in 1-point increments; Quantitative Reasoning, 130 to 170 in 1-point increments; and Analytical Writing, 0 to 6 in half-point increments. A section in which you answer no questions at all is reported as No Score (ETS, 2026). ETS gives no combined figure, so a school that asks for your GRE is reading the three scores it receives.

The same top score is not the same rank in both measures. ETS's percentile ranks, the percentage of test takers who scored lower, from everyone who tested between July 1, 2022, and June 30, 2025, put a Verbal 170 at the 99th percentile and a Quantitative 170 at the 89th; the means are 151.39 in Verbal and 157.62 in Quantitative (ETS, 2026). LSAT: 120 to 180

Your LSAT score starts as a raw score, the number of questions you answered correctly. Every question is weighted exactly the same, and there is no deduction for incorrect answers. The raw score is then converted to the LSAT scale, which ranges from 120 to 180, so that scores earned on different administrations can be compared (LSAC, 2026). LSAC publishes no section scores, so the single scaled number is the score.

LSAC's percentile rank is the percentage of test scores lower than a given score, over the 2023-2024, 2024-2025 and 2025-2026 testing years: 180 is 99.85, 175 is 98.72, 170 is 94.48, 165 is 85.17 and 160 is 71.06 (LSAC, 2026). Our guide to a good LSAT score covers what law schools report.

SAT: 400 to 1600

The SAT total score is the sum of the two section scores, Reading and Writing and Math, each 200 to 800, for a total of 400 to 1600 (College Board, 2026). The score report also shows a score range, how much your scores would likely vary on a different administration under identical conditions, and percentiles comparing you with other students who took the same test (College Board, 2026).

The other tests in the SAT Suite use smaller scales, so a PSAT score is not an SAT score: the PSAT/NMSQT and PSAT 10 total runs from 320 to 1520, and the PSAT 8/9 from 240 to 1440 (College Board, 2026). Our guide to a good SAT score has the percentiles.

ACT: 1 to 36

Each ACT section, English, math, reading and the optional science, is scored 1 to 36. Your raw score is the number of questions you answered correctly, with no penalty for incorrect answers, converted to a scale score so that results mean the same across test forms. The Composite is the average of your English, math and reading scores, rounded to the nearest whole number, with fractions of less than one-half rounded down and one-half or more rounded up (ACT, 2026).

The optional writing test is scored 2 to 12, the sum of two domain scores. ACT also reports a STEM score, the average of math and science, and an ELA score, the average of English, reading and writing if you took it (ACT, 2026). Can You Compare Scores Across Exams?

ACT and College Board developed official concordance tables for the ACT and the SAT, and ACT's example is that an ACT score of 30 is roughly equivalent to an SAT score of about 1370, while warning that the tables are estimates, not exact conversions (ACT, 2026). GMAC takes the opposite line for its exam: it says the GMAT has not been equated with other tests, that comparisons with scores from other tests, such as the GRE, are not appropriate, and that GMAT scores cannot be estimated from scores on other tests (GMAC, 2026).

So a number means something only on its own scale: 170 is the top of each GRE measure, while on the LSAT it sits 10 points below the top, at a percentile rank of 94.48 (LSAC, 2026). To judge a score, look at where it falls among the people who took the same test, which is what each test maker's percentiles are for, and at what the programs you want report on that test.
