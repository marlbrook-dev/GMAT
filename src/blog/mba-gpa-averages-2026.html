The average GPA for MBA programs runs from about 3.3 to 3.8: across 37 programs in our school library with a published figure for a class that entered in 2025 or 2026, the undergraduate GPA runs from 3.28 at Boston College Carroll to 3.76 at Harvard Business School and Stanford GSB (school class profiles and Poets&Quants, 2025 and 2026). 21 of the 37 are at 3.5 or higher. The ranges the same schools print matter as much: Chicago Booth's Class of 2027 runs from 2.5 to 4.0, so an average is the middle of a class, not a floor.

Averages, Medians and Means

29 of the 37 figures are averages. MIT Sloan, Yale SOM, Texas McCombs, Washington Foster, Cornell Johnson and Vanderbilt Owen report a median, and UCLA Anderson and UC San Diego Rady call theirs a mean, the same statistic as an average. A median is the GPA of the student in the middle of the class, so a few very high or very low GPAs move an average but not a median. Compare an average with an average, and check which class a figure describes: most here are the Class of 2027, which entered in 2025, and MIT Sloan's, UCLA Anderson's, Colorado Leeds's and Boston College Carroll's are the Class of 2028.

Is an Average GPA a Cutoff?

No. The ranges these schools print show admitted students well below their averages. Chicago Booth's Class of 2027 runs from 2.5 to 4.0, Northwestern Kellogg's from 2.7 to 4.0 and Dartmouth Tuck's from 2.8 to 4.0 (school class profiles, 2025). Where a school prints its middle 80 percent, about one student in ten sits below the bottom of that range: below 2.80 at Carnegie Mellon Tepper, 2.86 at Georgetown McDonough, 2.9 at UNC Kenan-Flagler and 2.97 at BU Questrom (school class profiles, 2025 and 2026). A GPA under a school's average is a reason to make the rest of the application carry more weight, not a reason to rule the school out.

Which GPAs Do the Averages Count?

Not always every student's. Harvard Business School's and Stanford GSB's averages are based on U.S. schools that use a 4.0 grading system, Wharton's average and MIT Sloan's median count only students from schools on a 4.0 system, and Chicago Booth's average covers domestic applicants (school class profiles, 2025 and 2026). A student whose university grades on another scale may not be in the figure at all, so an international applicant should read these as a guide to the rest of the class, and reading a class profile from outside the US covers what to look at instead.

How Should You Use These Numbers?

  • Set your GPA against a school's range or middle 80 percent where it prints one, not only its average.
  • Compare an average with an average and a median with a median, and check the class each figure describes.
  • Read the rest of the profile beside it: GMAT scores for top MBA programs and how to read an MBA class profile cover the numbers that sit next to GPA.
