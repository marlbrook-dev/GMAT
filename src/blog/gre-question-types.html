The GRE General Test asks seven types of question, three on Verbal Reasoning and four on Quantitative Reasoning, after a single Analytical Writing task. ETS puts the whole test at about 1 hour and 58 minutes in five sections: Analytical Writing always comes first, and the Verbal and Quantitative sections may appear in any order after it (ETS, 2026).

The Five Sections and Their Timing

SectionQuestionsTime

Quantitative Reasoning, section 21526 minutes

That is 54 questions in all, which works out to about a minute and a half for each Verbal question and about a minute and three quarters for each Quantitative one. Each measure adapts by section: ETS says the first section is of average difficulty and the difficulty of the second depends on your overall performance on the first, and within a section you can skip questions, mark them to come back to, and change answers. Our guide to adaptive testing compares that design with the GMAT's and the SAT's.

Verbal Reasoning: Three Question Types

ETS says about half of the Verbal measure asks you to read passages and answer questions on them, and the other half asks you to read, interpret and complete sentences, groups of sentences or paragraphs (ETS, 2026).

TypeWhat you do
Reading ComprehensionAnswer questions on a passage: one answer from five, every correct answer from three, or a sentence in the passage
Text CompletionFill the blanks in a passage of one to five sentences, choosing a word or short phrase for each
Sentence EquivalenceChoose the two of six choices that each complete a sentence, giving two sentences alike in meaning

Three of these formats give no credit for partially correct answers: Text Completion, Sentence Equivalence, and Reading Comprehension questions with one or more answers. A question counts only when every blank or choice in it is right.

Quantitative Reasoning: Four Question Types

ETS lists four types of question on the Quantitative measure, and each appears either on its own or in a Data Interpretation set, a group of questions on the same table, graph or other display of data (ETS, 2026). The content is arithmetic, algebra, geometry and data analysis, and an on-screen calculator is provided.

TypeWhat you do
Quantitative ComparisonDecide whether Quantity A or Quantity B is greater, the two are equal, or the relationship cannot be determined
Multiple choice, one answerSelect one answer from five
Multiple choice, one or more answersSelect every correct answer, or as many as the question asks for
Numeric EntryType an integer or a decimal, or a fraction in two boxes

A Data Interpretation set can use either kind of multiple choice and Numeric Entry, and our guide to Data Interpretation works through one.

Analytical Writing

The test opens with one Analyze an Issue task in 30 minutes. It is scored 0 to 6 in half-point increments and reported on its own, apart from the Verbal and Quantitative scores of 130 to 170 (ETS, 2026). Our guide to the essay on each test covers what the task asks.

How Should You Practice?

Learn the seven question types before test day, so no kind of question is new when it counts. ETS suggests its free POWERPREP Online tests for trying the test's design features, and our guide to free official practice tests lists them. The GRE trainer sorts its questions into seven categories, Reading Comprehension, Text Completion and Sentence Equivalence on Verbal and Arithmetic, Algebra, Geometry and Data Analysis on Quant, and its mock sections use ETS's question counts and times. Its quantitative questions are multiple choice with one answer or Quantitative Comparison, with a few Numeric Entry questions in its hand-written sets.
