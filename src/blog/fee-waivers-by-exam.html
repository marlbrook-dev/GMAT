Each of the five tests can cost less, or nothing, if you qualify. SAT and ACT fee waivers pay for up to two tests for eligible 11th and 12th graders (College Board, 2026; ACT, 2026), and LSAC's covers one or two LSATs by income tier (LSAC, 2026). ETS's Fee Reduction Program cuts the GRE General Test from $249 to $100 (ETS, 2026), and GMAT fee waivers typically come through business schools and approved programs (mba.com, 2026). Here is who qualifies, and how to apply.

GMAT: Through Business Schools and Programs

A GMAT fee waiver lets an eligible candidate register for the exam at a reduced cost or no cost. Fee waivers are typically distributed through participating business schools and approved programs, and the organization that issues one decides who is eligible and how it is handed out (mba.com, 2026). Vouchers are the other route: a voucher is a code applied during registration to reduce or cover the cost of an exam or service, and it can come from business schools, scholarship organizations, test prep organizations, GMAC Customer Care and other authorized organizations. Either one applies to the exam fee, which in the United States is $275 at a test center or $300 online (mba.com, 2026), but not to rescheduling fees, and neither is refunded, except as required by law (GMAC, 2026).

A few rules travel with them. Unless the issuer says otherwise, a voucher or fee waiver is for the recipient only, cannot be redeemed for cash or combined with other offers, and cannot be used once it has expired, and receiving a voucher does not guarantee an appointment (mba.com, 2026). One for the exam delivered online cannot be used for the exam at a test center, nor one for a test center online, so check which format yours covers before you book. Selling, redistributing or transferring one makes it fraudulent, and paying with a fraudulent voucher or fee waiver is among the reasons GMAC gives for canceling a score as a Serious Policy Violation, which also forfeits the test fees (GMAC, 2026). If you need a waiver, ask the admissions offices of the programs you are applying to. Our GMAT cost guide has every other GMAT fee.

GRE: The Fee Reduction Program

ETS's GRE Fee Reduction Program gives vouchers to four groups (ETS, 2026):

  • Financial need: US citizens or resident aliens planning to test in the United States, Puerto Rico, Guam or the US Virgin Islands who are college seniors receiving financial aid through an undergraduate college in the United States, US territories or Puerto Rico, or unenrolled college graduates who have applied for financial aid, and whose FAFSA Submission Summary shows a Student Aid Index (SAI) of zero or less.
  • Unemployment: a limited number of vouchers for US citizens or resident aliens aged 18 or older, testing in the same places, who became unemployed within the past six months, are receiving unemployment compensation and can submit an Unemployment Benefits Statement from the past 90 days.
  • Peace Corps: a limited number of vouchers for current volunteers, with a signed Description of Service on official Peace Corps letterhead that shows the dates of service. ETS cannot offer vouchers to people who have served before or plan to serve but are not volunteering now.
  • Programs: members of the national programs ETS lists that work with underrepresented groups, first-generation college students and students with financial need, among them QuestBridge, the Gates Millennium Scholars Program and the TRIO Ronald E. McNair Postbaccalaureate Achievement Program. Members ask their program coordinator for a voucher.

A voucher holder pays $100 for the General Test instead of $249, a saving of $149, and gets free access to POWERPREP PLUS Online Practice Tests 1 and 2 (valued at $45 each) and ScoreItNow! Online Writing Practice (valued at $20). A voucher can be used for one General Test, one Subject Test, or one of each, and on a Subject Test it takes half off the regular fee. To apply on financial need or unemployment, send ETS its GRE Fee Reduction Request Form; voucher numbers arrive by email within 2 weeks of approval. If your voucher came from a FAFSA Submission Summary submitted within the past year, you can apply for another one to retest (ETS, 2026). Our GRE cost guide has the rest of ETS's fees.

LSAT: Two Tiers Set by Income

LSAC's fee waiver is for prospective US and Canadian law students who are financially under-resourced. It places applicants in one of two tiers by tax-filing status and income, measured against the federal poverty guidelines (LSAC, 2026):

Tax-filing statusTier 1Tier 2
IndependentUp to 235% of the guidelines235 to 260%
Dependent, your incomeUp to 150%Up to 200%
Dependent, your and your parents' incomeUp to 200%200 to 300%

Income alone does not decide it. LSAC also sets maximum asset and cash balance levels, may weigh other factors, and says the income levels do not guarantee approval. The citizenship rules cover US citizens and nationals, permanent residents, and several other groups, among them people with Temporary Protected Status and those granted or applying for Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals (DACA); in Canada, citizens, permanent residents and protected persons or Convention refugees (LSAC, 2026).

You apply online in JD Services, then upload your supporting documents, such as your tax documents, within 45 days. While the application is conditionally approved, a hold keeps your LSAT scores and law school reports from being released. LSAC usually decides within two to five business days of receiving everything, and a denial can be appealed once. If an application is denied after conditional approval, LSAC bills you for any services you redeemed in the meantime. An approved waiver is valid for two years from its conditional approval. Tier 1 gives two LSATs, 6 law school reports, a five-year CAS subscription and a year of LawHub Advantage; Tier 2 gives one LSAT, 3 law school reports, the CAS subscription and LawHub Advantage. The LSAT arrives once you complete 2 PrepTests in LawHub in Exam Mode, and CAS once you complete LawHub's "How Do I Apply to Law School?" course. LSAC recommends applying at least six months before the registration deadline, and fees you have already paid cannot be waived retroactively (LSAC, 2026). SAT: From Your Counselor or From College Board

SAT fee waivers are for low-income 11th and 12th graders in the United States or its territories, and for US citizens living abroad. You are eligible if one or more of these apply to you (College Board, 2026):

  • You are enrolled in or eligible for the federal National School Lunch Program.
  • Your family's annual income falls within the Income Eligibility Guidelines set by the USDA Food and Nutrition Service.
  • You are enrolled in a federal, state or local program that aids students from low-income families, such as TRIO programs like Upward Bound.
  • Your family receives public assistance.
  • You are unhoused, or live in federally subsidized public housing or a foster home.
  • You are a ward of the state or an orphan.

There are two routes. Your school counselor can give you a fee waiver code, which you enter on your My SAT dashboard or when you register, or you can request a waiver directly from College Board with its request form, giving the contact details of an adult who can verify your eligibility. College Board asks for requests at least 1 to 2 weeks before the registration deadline, and says to expect an email with a status update within 1 to 2 weeks. The request form is not open to students living outside the United States or its territories, who ask their school for a code; homeschooled students can request directly. You can redeem one fee waiver code, or receive benefits directly from College Board, once per lifetime, and codes expire in August: those for the 2026-27 school year start with "FW26" (College Board, 2026).

The waiver gives two free SATs, unlimited score reports, waived application fees at participating colleges for seniors who have taken the SAT, and free CSS Profile applications at participating schools (College Board, 2026). It does not cover change fees, rush scores, the test center fee at select centers outside the US, or scores by phone (College Board, 2026). And College Board warns that giving false information about your eligibility may get your College Board account banned (College Board, 2026). Our SAT cost guide lists what each of those fees costs.

ACT: Through Your School Counselor

ACT's fee waiver is for students currently enrolled in 11th or 12th grade and testing in the United States, US territories or Puerto Rico who meet one or more indicators of economic need (ACT, 2026):

  • Enrolled in a federal free or reduced-price lunch program at school, based on USDA income levels.
  • Enrolled in a program for the economically disadvantaged, such as GEAR UP or Upward Bound.
  • Living in a foster home, a ward of the state, or homeless.
  • A family that receives low-income public assistance or lives in federally subsidized public housing.
  • A family whose total annual income is at or below the USDA levels for free or reduced-price lunches.

Taking part in one of those programs is not enough on its own: a student who participates but is not economically disadvantaged is not eligible. Your school counselor determines eligibility, and you enter the fee waiver code when you register, online or by mail. ACT supplies waivers to high schools, active Upward Bound projects and qualifying not-for-profit assistance organizations (ACT, 2026).

A waiver covers the registration fee for up to two ACT tests plus the science and writing add-ons, with a year of The Official ACT Self-Paced Course, ACT My Answer Key at no extra cost, and free score reports. It counts as used once you register, even if you do not test; moving it to a later date costs the change fee; you can use at most two; and it cannot refund a fee you have already paid, or pay for other fees, products or services. Supplies are limited (ACT, 2026). Our ACT cost guide has the change fee and the rest.

Apply Before You Book

The timing rules all point the same way. LSAC recommends applying at least six months before the registration deadline, College Board asks for requests at least 1 to 2 weeks before it, and ETS sends voucher numbers within 2 weeks of approval, so apply before you book a test date, not after. A waiver does not reach back, either: fees you have already paid to LSAC cannot be waived retroactively, and an ACT waiver cannot refund a test fee you have paid (LSAC, 2026; ACT, 2026). Have the documents each program asks for ready when you apply, such as your FAFSA Submission Summary for ETS or your tax documents for LSAC.

Whichever test you are preparing for, the free trainers for the GMAT, GRE, LSAT, SAT and ACT run an adaptive round with no account needed, and report an estimated range from your practice, never a predicted score.
