All five test makers give away official full-length practice tests. GMAC's free GMAT Official Starter Kit includes Practice Exams 1 and 2 (GMAC, 2026). ETS offers two free POWERPREP Online tests for the GRE, one untimed and one timed (ETS, 2026), and LSAC makes four full LSAT PrepTests free on LawHub (LSAC, 2026). College Board's full-length SAT practice tests are free in the Bluebook app (College Board, 2026), and ACT offers four full-length practice tests, digital or paper, always free (ACT, 2026). Here is what each one includes, what it leaves out, and what costs extra.

GMAT: Two Free Practice Exams in the Official Starter Kit

GMAC's free GMAT Official Starter Kit includes Free Practice Exams 1 & 2, sample practice questions and access to the Official Prep platform, where the two exams sit under GMAT Full-Length Exams (GMAC, 2026). The exams use retired GMAT questions and the same scoring algorithm as the GMAT exam, adapt question difficulty based on your performance, and give Total and Section scores with a detailed score report. You get the score report only if you complete and submit the exam (GMAC, 2026).

Two limits are worth knowing before you start. Answer explanations are not available for GMAT Official Practice Exams, which are designed to replicate the real test; GMAC points candidates who want explanations to its Practice Questions products instead (GMAC, 2026). And the free exams can be taken more than once, but after the first two attempts you are increasingly likely to meet questions you have already seen, and scores from repeated attempts may no longer be as valid as earlier ones (GMAC, 2026). GMAC's other official practice exams, 3 to 6, are sold through its Official Prep Store, with two attempts per exam (GMAC, 2026).

GRE: Two Free POWERPREP Online Tests

ETS offers two free POWERPREP Online practice tests, both full-length. POWERPREP 1 is untimed, and because it is untimed it gives no Verbal Reasoning or Quantitative Reasoning scores; POWERPREP 2 is timed. Each comes with the correct answers to the Verbal Reasoning and Quantitative Reasoning questions, scored Analytical Writing sample essays with rater commentary, and a report that summarizes your performance (ETS, 2026).

POWERPREP PLUS Online costs $44.95 per practice test. It comes with instant scoring with detailed score reports, scored Analytical Writing essays with expert commentary, and explanations and difficulty levels for all question types (ETS, 2026). For a shorter first look, ETS also has a free 10-question GRE Mini Quiz (ETS, 2026).

LSAT: Four Free PrepTests on LawHub

LSAC makes four full four-section LSAT PrepTests available for free through LawHub. They run in a simulation of the LSAT in which you can do everything you can do during the actual test: rule out answers, highlight passages, set screen preferences and more (LSAC, 2026). A LawHub account is free; LawHub Advantage costs $124 and gives one year of access to an extensive library of full Official LSAT PrepTests (LSAC, 2026).

LSAC's own advice on using them is about time. Taking practice tests under time constraints, it says, will help you estimate how much time you can afford to spend on each question, and identify the question types with which you need more practice (LSAC, 2026).

SAT: Free Practice Tests in Bluebook, and on Paper

College Board's official full-length SAT practice tests are free in Bluebook, the app the SAT itself is taken in (College Board, 2026). They are timed like a real test, except that you can move on from one section to the next before time expires, and they are scored: afterwards you can view and download your scores in My Practice, review questions, and get targeted practice through Khan Academy. Bluebook also has a short test preview, which is untimed and gives no scores (College Board, 2026).

The same tests can be downloaded free as PDFs, each with a scoring guide and answer explanations, but the paper versions are nonadaptive, and College Board recommends them only for students who will test with paper-based accommodations. Students testing in Bluebook should take at least one adaptive practice test in the app before test day, it says (College Board, 2026). Beyond the full tests, the free Official SAT Prep on Khan Academy and the Student Question Bank each hold thousands of practice questions (College Board, 2026).

ACT: Four Free Practice Tests, Digital or Paper

ACT offers four official full-length practice tests, Practice Tests 1 to 4, and calls them "digital or paper, always free." The digital version of each is an interactive simulation with a scoring guide and diagnostic tools, whose English, math, reading, science and writing sections can each be taken timed or untimed; the paper version is a downloadable booklet with a scoring key (ACT, 2026). Each online section is completed in one sitting, and closing the page erases your responses; after a digital test, you convert your raw score with the scoring key (ACT, 2026).

Science and writing are optional on the ACT itself (ACT, 2026), so practice the sections you registered for. Screen-reader and text-to-speech versions of every section are available timed and untimed, and ACT's free prep page adds QuizMe practice quizzes and, with a free MyACT account, a Question of the Day (ACT, 2026).

How to Get the Most From a Handful of Tests

These are small numbers: two free full-length tests for the GMAT and the GRE, four for the LSAT and the ACT. Spend them deliberately. Take one early, under real timing, to see where you start, and keep the rest as checkpoints for after your preparation has changed something. GMAC's caution about its own exams is worth applying to all of them: repeated exposure to questions can improve performance through familiarity, so a second score on the same test may not reflect the same conditions as the first (GMAC, 2026).

Between full tests, the practice that moves a score is targeted work on your weakest skills, which is what our study plan template is built around, and our guide to how long each test runs shows how much time to set aside for a full sitting.
