The GRE General Test runs about 1 hour and 58 minutes in five sections: one Analytical Writing task, two Verbal Reasoning sections and two Quantitative Reasoning sections, for 54 questions plus the essay (ETS, 2026). ETS reports three scores, Verbal and Quant each from 130 to 170 and the essay from 0 to 6, and no total. This guide covers what each section tests, how scoring works, and how to prepare.

What Sections Does the GRE Have?

Analytical Writing always comes first. The two Verbal Reasoning and two Quantitative Reasoning sections may follow in any order, and in each measure the second section is the longer one (ETS, 2026).

All figures describe the GRE General Test as ETS has given it since September 22, 2023. Verbal Reasoning asks three types of question: Reading Comprehension, Text Completion and Sentence Equivalence, and ETS says about half of the measure asks you to read passages and answer questions on them. Two of the three have no GMAT counterpart. Text Completion asks you to fill the blanks in a short passage, and Sentence Equivalence asks for the two answer choices that each complete a sentence, giving two sentences alike in meaning. Quantitative Reasoning covers arithmetic, algebra, geometry and data analysis, with an on-screen calculator, and geometry is the area the GMAT Focus Edition no longer tests (mba.com, GMAC, 2026).

How Is the GRE Scored and Reported?

You receive three scores, not one: Verbal Reasoning and Quantitative Reasoning, each 130 to 170 in 1-point increments, and Analytical Writing, 0 to 6 in half-point increments (ETS, 2026). ETS reports no total. Adding Verbal and Quant gives a figure from 260 to 340, but ETS does not report that sum and publishes no percentile for it, so read each score against its own percentile rank.

Percentiles carry the meaning. The same scaled number can sit at very different percentile ranks in Verbal and in Quant, and our GRE score calculator gives each score's rank from ETS's own table. For a sense of where selective programs land, the Harvard Business School Class of 2027 reported a median GRE of 164 Quant and 164 Verbal (HBS Class of 2027 profile, 2025).

Score reporting is flexible in ways the scales are not. ETS lets you choose which scores schools see, and GRE scores are reportable for 5 years following your test date (ETS, 2026). One caution for MBA applicants: never compare a GRE number to a GMAT number directly. The scales share nothing, so compare percentiles, and use our GMAT vs GRE comparison when choosing between the exams.

Is the GRE Adaptive?

Yes, at the section level. ETS says the first section of each measure is of average difficulty and the difficulty of the second depends on your overall performance on the first, and that scoring takes into account both the number of questions answered correctly across the two sections and their difficulty. Within a section you can skip a question, mark it to come back to, and change answers until the section clock runs out. The GMAT Focus Edition takes the opposite approach, adapting question by question, with bookmarking and up to 3 answer changes allowed per section (mba.com, GMAC, 2026).

The practical consequence is a difference in pacing strategy. On the GRE, the first section of each measure sets the difficulty of the second, so early questions deserve full care, and free movement within a section makes a two pass approach possible: answer what comes quickly, then circle back to what does not.

Who Might Choose the GRE?

Acceptance need not decide it. Harvard Business School, for one, says there is no minimum GMAT or GRE to apply and that it has no preference towards a particular test, and 44% of its Class of 2027 submitted the GRE, against 34% the GMAT and 28% the GMAT 10th edition, with some students submitting more than one (HBS Class of 2027 profile, 2025). Beyond that, these reasons point to the GRE:

  • Multiple applications. One GRE score can support an MBA application and an application to a master's program in another field in the same cycle.
  • Words as well as logic. Text Completion and Sentence Equivalence ask you to choose the words that complete a passage or sentence, and the GMAT Focus Edition has no question of that kind; its Verbal section is reading comprehension and critical reasoning.
  • Calculator comfort. The GRE provides an on-screen calculator for its Quantitative Reasoning sections. The GMAT Focus Edition allows one only in Data Insights (mba.com, GMAC, 2026).
  • Writers. The GRE keeps a scored essay, which suits applicants who want a writing sample on record. The GMAT Focus Edition has none.

If several of these describe you, the GRE may be your exam. If none do, take one timed practice test for each and let the percentile results make the call.

How Should You Prepare for the GRE Format?

The format is particular; the method is not. Start with a full-length practice test, train in timed sets, log every miss, and review those misses on a spaced schedule so skills still hold on test day.

Split content work into three lanes matching the three measures. For Quant, rebuild arithmetic, algebra, geometry and data analysis, and practice with the on-screen calculator so it speeds you up instead of tempting you into brute force. For Verbal, pair steady reading practice with a vocabulary review deck that resurfaces old words on a schedule. For Analytical Writing, draft full essays under the clock rather than reading about essays. Then rehearse the section adaptive rhythm with ETS's free POWERPREP Online practice tests, which ETS suggests for trying the test's design features.

If you are arriving from GMAT prep, arithmetic, algebra, word problems and reading comprehension all carry over. What you must add is Text Completion, Sentence Equivalence, geometry and the essay, so budget several weeks for format-specific work. The Bottom Line

The GRE General Test is a section adaptive exam of about 1 hour and 58 minutes: one Analyze an Issue task, then two Verbal and two Quantitative sections, 54 questions in all, with free movement inside each section (ETS, 2026). Verbal and Quant each report on a 130 to 170 scale and the essay on a 0 to 6 scale, with no total, and percentiles do the real talking. Pick the exam whose percentiles favor you, prepare with timed practice and spaced review, and confirm the operational details on ets.org before you book a seat.
