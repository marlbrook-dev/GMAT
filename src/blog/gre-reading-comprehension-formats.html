GRE Reading Comprehension asks questions about passages in three formats: choose one answer from five, choose every correct answer from three, or click the sentence in the passage that meets a description. ETS says that typically about half of the Verbal Reasoning questions are based on passages, each passage has from one to six questions, and most passages are one paragraph long, with one or two that run to several (ETS, 2026).

What the Passages Are

ETS says the passages are based on material from books and periodicals, academic and nonacademic, in the physical, biological and social sciences, business, the arts and humanities, and everyday topics. No specialized knowledge is assumed: every question can be answered from the passage, and if a passage seems particularly hard or unfamiliar, ETS suggests you may want to save it for last.

How to Read a Passage

ETS's reading tips come down to telling the parts of a passage apart:

  • Separate the main ideas from the supporting ideas or evidence.
  • Separate the ideas the author advances from those the author merely reports, and the ideas the author is strongly committed to from those offered as hypothetical or speculative.
  • Find the main transitions and how the ideas relate: whether they contrast or are consistent, whether one supports another, and whether one spells out another in more detail or applies it to a particular case.
  • Answer from the passage, not from what you know, even where your own views differ from the author's.

The Three Formats

Select one answer. Five choices, one correct. ETS's advice is to read every choice before selecting, even if you think you know the answer. The answer is the one that most accurately and most completely answers the question, so beware of choices that are only partially true or only partially answer it, and do not pick a choice simply because it is a true statement. When a question asks what a word means, choose the sense in which the passage uses it.

Select one or more answers. Three choices, of which one, two or all three may be correct, and you must select all the correct ones to gain credit, with no credit for partially correct answers. ETS advises judging each choice on its own merits without regard to the others, and not to be disturbed if all three seem correct, since all three can be.

Select a sentence. You click the sentence in the passage that meets a description; in longer passages the question usually applies only to one or two specified paragraphs. ETS advises evaluating each sentence in those paragraphs separately and not selecting a sentence if any part of the description does not apply to it, though the description may not cover every aspect of the sentence. Because the format depends on the computer, the paper-delivered test uses equivalent multiple-choice questions instead.

A Worked Example

For years the decline of one valley's orchards was blamed on a run of cold springs. The blossom records kept by the growers' society tell a different story. Frost did damage the blossom in three of the worst years, but the decline went on through the warm springs that followed. What changed in those years was the price of land: as the valley's towns grew, growers sold their lower orchards for housing and kept only the hillside trees, which bear less. Cold springs may explain a bad year; they cannot explain a falling trend.

1. The passage is primarily concerned with (A) explaining a long decline that had been put down to the weather (B) describing how frost damages blossom (C) arguing that housing should not be built on orchard land (D) comparing the soils of the valley and the hillsides (E) defending the growers' society's records. The passage sets out the old explanation, the evidence against it and a new one, so the answer is (A). (B) is one detail, (C) and (D) are never argued, and (E) mistakes using the records for defending them.

2. Which of the following does the passage indicate about the valley's orchards? Indicate all that apply. (A) Frost damaged the blossom in some of the worst years. (B) The decline continued through warm springs. (C) The hillside trees bear more than the lower orchards did. The passage states (A) and (B), and says the reverse of (C), so the answer is (A) and (B), both of them.

3. Select the sentence in which the author gives an alternative explanation for the decline. The answer is the fourth sentence, the one that begins "What changed in those years". The second only announces that the records disagree, the third gives evidence against the old account, and the last draws the conclusion rather than giving the explanation.

Common Traps

  • A true statement that does not answer the question. ETS warns against picking a choice simply because it is true.
  • Stopping at one correct choice in a select one or more question. Each choice is judged on its own, and all three can be correct.
  • Selecting a sentence that meets most of a description. If any part of the description does not apply, it is the wrong sentence.

How Should You Practice?

For each passage, say its main point and the author's position in a sentence before the first question.
