The SAT and ACT differ most in pace, structure, and scales. The digital SAT runs 2 hours 14 minutes with 98 questions and a 400 to 1600 total (College Board, 2025). The enhanced ACT's core test, English, Math, and Reading, runs 125 minutes with 131 questions, scored 1 to 36, with Science and Writing now optional add-ons (act.org and Applerouth, 2025). Both cost $68 to register for the 2025-26 year. Colleges that use scores accept either, so the choice comes down to which format fits you.

The Pace Difference Is the Real Difference

Run the arithmetic and the tests separate cleanly. The SAT gives about 82 seconds per question over its 98 questions; the ACT's core sections give about 57 seconds per question over 131. A one-sentence definition for the deciding factor: time pressure tolerance is how much of your accuracy survives when the clock, not the content, becomes the constraint. Students whose accuracy holds at speed often prefer the ACT's sprint; students who convert extra seconds into caught mistakes usually score better on the SAT. A timed practice section of each, scored honestly, settles the question faster than any opinion.

Scoring Details That Change Decisions

Two recent structural changes matter more than folklore. First, the enhanced ACT's Composite now averages English, Math, and Reading only, with Science optional and reported separately (Applerouth, 2025), so a science-heavy profile no longer props up, or drags down, the headline number. Second, the SAT has been fully digital in the US since March 2024 (College Board, 2024), so paper-based practice materials misstate the experience; practice in the same digital format you will test in.

Choosing, Then Committing

Pick with a two-week experiment: one timed digital SAT section set and one timed ACT core set, same week, same conditions, then compare percentile performance rather than raw feel. After that, commit; the tests overlap enough that split preparation dilutes both.
