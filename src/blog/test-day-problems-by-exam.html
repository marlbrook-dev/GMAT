Most test days go as planned. When one does not, each test maker has written down what happens next, and the remedies differ. GMAC may offer a free retest, a paid retest or a refund when it cancels a score for a Testing Issue (GMAC, 2026). ETS offers a free new appointment or a full refund, and lets you seek your travel costs, when a technical problem cancels a GRE at a test center (ETS, 2026). A remote LSAT pauses while you reconnect (LSAC, 2026), College Board may offer a makeup SAT after a misadministration (College Board, 2026), and ACT decides, in its sole discretion, whether to correct the error, cancel scores, or offer a free retest or a refund (ACT, 2026). Here is each rule, and what to do on the day.

GMAT: Tell the Proctor, Then GMAC Within Seven Days

Testing online, you must notify the proctor of any technical issues you experience during the exam immediately; it is one of the user agreements you accept before the exam starts (GMAC, 2026). After the exam, the window is seven days. If you experienced a disruption at a test center and believe it affected your performance, report the incident in person at the test center, or contact GMAC Customer Care within seven days of your exam date. If you tested online, submit concerns about technical issues to Customer Care within the same seven days. GMAC says it will research your concern as quickly as possible and respond (GMAC, 2026).

GMAC can also cancel a score itself for a Testing Issue, an event that affects the administration of an exam, such as an administrative or technical error. A score canceled this way is noted on the Official Score Report as a T, and schools may be notified if the score was already reported. GMAC may then offer, in its sole discretion, a retest at no additional charge, a retest for the test fee, or a refund of the test fee. A retest means retaking the entire exam, and GMAC may require it at a test center. The canceled session still counts toward the limit of five GMAT attempts in a rolling 12-month period (GMAC, 2026), which our guide to retake limits covers.

GRE: A Free Retest or a Full Refund, Plus Travel Costs

ETS's promise is the most specific. If a technical problem at the test center makes it necessary to cancel your test session, or if it is later determined that your scores could not be reported, you are offered another test appointment free of charge or a full refund of the original test fee. You may also seek reimbursement from ETS for reasonable and documented expenses of traveling to the test center, even though, as ETS puts it, technical problems are generally not its fault (ETS, 2026).

To claim, contact GRE Services within 30 days of your original appointment with your name, date of birth, mailing address, daytime telephone number, email address, original test date and registration appointment number, and a brief description of what occurred at the test center. Weather or other circumstances beyond the test administrator's or ETS's control can also delay the start of a test or require it to be rescheduled (ETS, 2026).

LSAT: A Remote Test Pauses While You Reconnect

LSAC's answers cover a dropped connection directly. If you lose your internet connection or have other technical difficulties, in most cases the remote proctoring system saves your progress and pauses the test while the issue is resolved, and when you restart, you resume where you left off. After an interruption you must complete the security check-in process again before resuming, and the proctor decides whether to let you resume more than once if the connection keeps failing. If you cannot restore your connection during your scheduled testing window, the session is terminated and you are moved to a future registration date (LSAC, 2026).

Interruptions in the room are covered too. If someone enters, ask them to leave immediately; at the proctor's discretion you may continue once they have gone, and the session is flagged for further review, but you are not penalized if the interaction is brief and it is evident that no information about the test was communicated. A pet that wanders in is not a problem: you may continue to test (LSAC, 2026).

SAT: Raise Your Hand, and a Makeup If the Test Goes Wrong

On the digital SAT, the first step is in the room or on screen: if you have a problem during the test, raise your hand to ask the proctor for help or click the Help icon for troubleshooting tips (College Board, 2026). College Board's advice for breaks is to leave your device open rather than close it, and an unscheduled break comes out of your testing time (College Board, 2026).

If the problem is bigger than one student's screen, College Board may offer a makeup test when a test center is unable to open on test day or a misadministration or irregularity occurs, and it contacts you if a makeup is needed. At a makeup you may take only the tests you registered for on the original date, at the center for which you are registered or authorized, and you must take the entire test (College Board, 2026).

ACT: ACT Decides, and Its Decision Is Final

ACT's terms list what can go wrong: a mistiming, events that cause testing to be canceled or interrupted, concerns about whether testing can be safely conducted, errors, delays or technical difficulties anywhere from registration to score reporting, and disruptions at the test location. When one occurs, ACT examines the situation and may decide that no further action is necessary, a decision that is final. If it does act, it chooses, in its sole discretion, to correct the error where that is feasible, to cancel scores, or to discontinue the testing process and offer each affected person either a retest at no additional fee, normally on a future test date, or a refund (ACT, 2026).

State testing works differently. If a state or district paid for your test, ACT may offer a retest at no additional fee, or it may cancel the test event without an option to retest (ACT, 2026).

If Something Goes Wrong on Your Test Day

Two habits cover most of these rules. First, raise the problem at the time: GMAC requires online test takers to tell the proctor immediately, and College Board's first advice is to raise your hand. Second, know the deadline that follows: seven days to report a GMAT disruption or technical concern, and 30 days to claim GRE travel costs after a technical problem cancels a session at a test center. A problem on test day is not the same as missing the test; our guide to what happens if you miss a test covers that, and our guide to canceling a score covers the decision that is yours rather than the test maker's.

Whichever test you are preparing for, the free trainers for the GMAT, GRE, LSAT, SAT and ACT run an adaptive round with no account needed, and report an estimated range from your practice, never a predicted score.
