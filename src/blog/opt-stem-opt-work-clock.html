An MBA in the United States buys two things: the degree, and a period of time in which you are allowed to work. Applicants research the first exhaustively and the second barely at all. This is the arithmetic of the second.

Pre Completion OPT Is Borrowed Time, Not Extra Time

You may use OPT before finishing your degree as well as after. What the rules make clear, and what students routinely miss, is that the 12 months is a single budget. Time spent on pre completion OPT reduces post completion eligibility.

Pre completion OPT is also capped at 20 hours or less per week while school is in session, and is only available after one full academic year of lawful enrollment. It can be full time when school is not in session. For most MBA students the internship between the two years is handled through CPT rather than OPT for exactly this reason: spending the OPT budget during the degree leaves less of it for the part that matters, which is the job after.

The Filing Windows Are Narrow and They Are Not Suggestions

For post completion OPT there are three constraints at once:

  • You may apply up to 90 days before you complete your degree.
  • You may apply no later than 60 days after you complete it.
  • You must apply within 30 days after your designated school official enters the OPT recommendation into your SEVIS record.

That third one is the trap. The clock starts when your DSO acts, not when you decide to act. If you ask for the recommendation and then take six weeks to file, the recommendation window has closed regardless of where you sit in the 90 and 60 day windows.

For the STEM extension, file Form I-765 up to 90 days before your current OPT authorization expires and within 60 days of the DSO's SEVIS recommendation. File on time and, if your OPT expires while the application is pending, employment authorization is automatically extended for 180 days.

Unemployment Days Are a Budget You Are Spending Without Noticing

On post completion OPT you may accrue up to 90 days of unemployment. A STEM extension adds 60 more, for 150 in total across the whole period.

Ninety days sounds generous until you place it against the calendar. Your OPT start date is set when the authorization is approved, not when you find a job. If you graduate in May, start OPT in July, and are still searching in October, you have spent most of the budget. The practical consequence is that the job search has a hard deadline attached to it that has nothing to do with your finances or your patience.

Note also that on post completion OPT you must work part time of at least 20 hours per week, or full time. An unpaid or very part time arrangement may not stop the unemployment clock.

Where the 36 Months Actually Goes

Here is the reason the STEM question is worth the email it takes to ask. The H-1B registration window opens once a year. With 12 months of authorization you are in exactly one lottery. With 36 you are in three, and if you are not selected the first time you have not lost the country.

The regular H-1B cap is 65,000 new statuses per fiscal year, with an additional 20,000 for holders of a master's degree or higher from a US institution, per USCIS. Your US MBA qualifies you for that second allocation, which is a genuine advantage of studying in the United States. It does not make selection certain.

Everything here depends on whether your degree is on the DHS STEM list, which depends on a four digit code. That is covered in the CIP code piece, and it is the single highest leverage thing to sort out before you apply.

One Recent Change to Fold In

Since September 15, 2026, the window to prepare to depart after an authorized period of post completion practical training ends is 30 days rather than 60. The rule change piece covers what else moved. The short version for this article: the end of your OPT is now a harder stop than it used to be, which makes the 150 unemployment days and the filing windows matter more, not less.
