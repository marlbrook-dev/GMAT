A good GRE score depends on your programs, but ETS percentiles set the national scale: a 160 in Verbal Reasoning is higher than 82 percent of test takers' scores, while a 160 in Quantitative Reasoning is higher than only 50 percent (ETS, 2026). Quant scores run higher across the board, with a mean of 157.62 against 151.39 in Verbal, so the same number means more in Verbal. Even a perfect 170 in Quant is the 89th percentile.

What Does a GRE Percentile Mean?

A GRE percentile rank is the percentage of test takers in the reference group who obtained scores lower than yours (ETS, 2026). That is a stricter definition than some tests use: ACT's national ranks count everyone at or below a score. So a Quant 170 at the 89th percentile means 89 percent scored lower and the rest, about 11 percent, scored 170 too. The reference group for scores reported now is everyone who tested between July 1, 2022, and June 30, 2025: 788,021 people in Verbal Reasoning and 790,864 in Quantitative Reasoning.

Why Is the Same Score Worth More in Verbal?

Because the two measures have different distributions. The Verbal mean is 151.39 with a standard deviation of 8.43, and the Quant mean is 157.62 with a standard deviation of 9.90 (ETS, 2026). A 165 therefore sits at the 95th percentile in Verbal and the 67th in Quant, so a strong Verbal score and a strong Quant score are different numbers.

What GRE Score Should You Aim For?

Set the target from your programs, then check it against the percentiles. Business schools make a useful example: Harvard Business School reports a median of 164 Verbal and 164 Quantitative for its Class of 2027 (HBS Class of 2027 profile, 2025), which on the ETS tables is the 93rd percentile in Verbal and the 63rd in Quant.
