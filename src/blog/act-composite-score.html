Your ACT Composite score is the average of your English, math and reading scores, rounded to the nearest whole number: fractions less than one-half round down and fractions of one-half or more round up (ACT, 2026). Science no longer counts. So English 28, math 31 and reading 30 add to 89, average 29.67, and report as a Composite of 30. The optional science and writing tests are scored and reported separately.

How Is the ACT Composite Calculated?

Add the three section scores, each on the 1 to 36 scale, divide by three, and round. The Composite is the average of your English, math and reading scores, as ACT's score guide puts it. A few worked examples show how the rounding lands.

Why Is the Fraction Never Exactly One Half?

An average of three whole numbers can only end in zero, one third or two thirds, because any whole-number sum divided by three leaves a remainder of 0, 1 or 2. The one-half case in ACT's rounding rule therefore never arises on the current Composite: a third always rounds down and two thirds always rounds up. A useful term follows. A Composite band is the set of section-score sums that report the same Composite, and each band holds three sums: 89, 90 and 91 all report as 30, and 92 begins the band for 31.

The practical consequence is that one more section point moves the Composite a full point when your sum is the top of its band, as 91 is, and does nothing when your sum is the bottom of its band, as 89 is. Every section counts equally, so the cheapest point is whichever section is easiest for you to raise.

What Happened to Science?

ACT started phasing science out of the Composite for national online testing in April 2025, and in September 2025 all ACT tests, in every mode, moved to the updated Composite (ACT, 2026). Science is now an optional section. It is still scored 1 to 36 and feeds a STEM score, the average of math and science, while an ELA score averages English, reading and writing when writing is taken. The optional writing test is scored 2 to 12 as the sum of two domain scores and does not enter the Composite either.

How Does ACT Superscoring Work?

ACT defines the superscore as the average of your best score in each section across your test attempts, and it applies the same method as the Composite: for the enhanced ACT, add your best English, math and reading, divide by three, and round (ACT superscore FAQ, 2026). ACT's own example takes English 32 from one date, math 28 from another and reading 30 from a third, for a superscore Composite of 30. The superscore is calculated automatically once you have tested more than once since September 2016, and the optional science and writing tests are not part of it. Whether a college uses it is the college's decision, so check each admissions office's own testing policy.

What Should You Do With This?

Treat the Composite as three equal levers. Find your current sum, see where it sits in its band, and put the next week of practice into the section where a point is cheapest.
