Women make up a little under half of most MBA classes. Across 35 programs in our school library with a published figure for a class that entered in 2025 or 2026, women are from 24 to 56 percent of the class, and the middle program's class is 43 percent women (school class profiles and Poets&Quants, 2025 and 2026). 21 of the 35 are at 40 percent or more, and four are at half or more.

Where Are Women Half the Class?

At four programs in the table: Lehigh, where the class that entered in 2025 is 56 percent female, UC San Diego Rady at 52 percent, Washington Foster at 51 percent and College of Charleston's one-year cohort at 50 percent (school class profiles, 2025). At the other end, Vanderbilt Owen's Class of 2027 is 24 percent female, Boston College Carroll's Class of 2028 is 30 percent women and USC Marshall's Class of 2027 is 31 percent (school class profiles, 2025 and 2026). Many sit in a narrow band: Harvard Business School, Wharton, Yale SOM and Dartmouth Tuck are at 44 percent, Stanford GSB and NYU Stern at 45 percent, and Columbia at 46 percent (school class profiles and Poets&Quants, 2025).

Women or Female?

Schools differ in the word: some say women, others female, and there can be a reason. MIT Sloan's class profile explains that the federal IPEDS methodology, which universities must use when reporting student data to the U.S. Department of Education, now directs them to report legal sex only, replacing women with female (MIT Sloan, 2026). Either way, each figure in the table is a share of the class the school enrolled, not of its applicants or admits.

How Should You Use These Numbers?

  • Read a share as one class's: the table names the class each figure describes, and a small program's share can move several points in a year.
  • Set it beside the rest of the profile: average GPA for MBA programs and how to read an MBA class profile cover the numbers next to it.
  • Ask the school about what the profile does not show, such as clubs, fellowships and outcomes by gender, where it publishes them.
