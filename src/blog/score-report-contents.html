A score report carries more than the score. GMAC's Official Score Report adds percentile rankings and performance insights, and for repeat takers the GMAT Superscore (GMAC, 2026). ETS sends institutions only the scores you choose to report, with no sign of any other GRE test (ETS, 2026). LSAC's report lists every reportable LSAT result, up to 12, with a percentile rank for each score (LSAC, 2026). College Board's shows a Score Range and a breakdown by content area (College Board, 2026), and ACT's adds STEM and ELA scores and the questions you got right in each reporting category (ACT, 2026). Here is what each report shows, and what the schools you send it to receive.

GMAT: Scores, Insights and the Superscore

The GMAT Official Score Report includes detailed performance insights at no additional cost: in addition to your total and section scores and percentile rankings, it gives insights by section, question type and content domain, and details on time management (mba.com, 2026). Since August 12, 2026, a candidate with more than one attempt may also have a GMAT Superscore, built from the best section scores across attempts, which goes to schools automatically with any single attempt score you send (GMAC, 2026), as our guide to which scores schools see explains.

What a program receives is set out by GMAC. Each Official Score Report includes basic information from your mba.com profile, your GMAT exam scores, and any self-reported information you chose to provide, such as your undergraduate GPA or intended area of study; a report of a GMAT Exam, 10th Edition score also carries your Analytical Writing Assessment essay (GMAC, 2026). There is no PDF: GMAC does not offer a physical or downloadable version of the score report, and a screenshot of your score is not an official score report (GMAC, 2026).

GRE: What You See and What Schools See

ETS keeps two reports apart. Your Test-taker Score Report, in your ETS account, holds your contact information, date of birth, gender, intended graduate major, test dates, scores with their percentile ranks, the institutions you have sent scores to, and a record of scores reported in the last 5 years (ETS, 2026). Verbal Reasoning and Quantitative Reasoning are each reported from 130 to 170 and Analytical Writing from 0 to 6, and a measure where no questions were answered gets a No Score (NS) (ETS, 2026).

The Institution Score Report a school receives carries your contact information, date of birth, gender, intended graduate major field, and the test dates and scores you choose to report with ScoreSelect, with their percentile ranks. It does not include the other schools you have chosen, the scores you chose not to report, or any indication that you have taken other GRE tests. Schools can, though, see your photos and essay responses from each administration you report, in the ETS Data Manager (ETS, 2026).

LSAT: Every Reportable Result, With a Score Band

LSAC's score report includes your current score, the results of all reportable tests, up to 12, including absences and cancellations, your percentile rank for each score, and your score band (LSAC, 2026). The percentile rank is the percentage of test takers whose scores were lower than yours over the previous three testing years. The score itself runs from 120 to 180 and rests on the number of questions you answered correctly, every question weighted the same, with no deduction for incorrect answers (LSAC, 2026).

SAT: A Score Range and Eight Content Areas

The SAT score report shows three scores, the total and the Reading and Writing and Math section scores, and beside them a Score Range, which shows how much your scores would likely vary if you took a different administration under identical conditions, and percentiles that compare you with other students who took the same test. A Knowledge and Skills section then shows how you did in each of the eight content areas the test measures, four in each section, with the approximate number of questions in each (College Board, 2026). Students in the United States also see a Career Insights Snapshot, a list of growing careers in their state that connect to their scores, which College Board calls examples, not recommendations (College Board, 2026).

ACT: STEM, ELA and Reporting Categories

An ACT score report gives your Composite and your section scores, each from 1 to 36, and a writing score from 2 to 12 if you took writing. It adds a STEM score, the average of math and science, and an ELA score, the average of English, reading and writing, if taken. Each reporting category shows the number of questions in it, the number you answered correctly and your percentage correct (ACT, 2026). Your Percentile Can Change When Your Score Does Not

Percentiles are recalculated, and a report can change under you. GMAC updates its percentile tables every year: your total score stays the same, and your Official Score Report is updated with the revised percentiles (GMAC, 2026). LSAC updates the percentiles for all reported scores by the end of July each year (LSAC, 2026). Three of the five makers also say their reports do not mark accommodations: GMAC, LSAC and ACT each state that score reports do not indicate testing with accommodations (GMAC, 2026; LSAC, 2026; ACT, 2026), as our guide to testing accommodations explains. Whichever test you are preparing for, the free trainers for the GMAT, GRE, LSAT, SAT and ACT run an adaptive round with no account needed, and report an estimated range from your practice, never a predicted score.
