Four of the five tests include free score reports, and each sets a deadline for using them. The GMAT gives you five, to send within 48 hours of your Official Score appearing (mba.com, 2026). The GRE gives four, chosen on test day (ETS, 2026). The SAT gives four, to use by nine days after a weekend test (College Board, 2026), and the ACT's price covers four colleges named when you register (ACT, 2026). The LSAT includes none: every law school application needs a $45 law school report (LSAC, 2026). Here is how each works and what a report costs once the free ones are gone.

GMAT: Five Reports Within 48 Hours

Your GMAT registration includes sending your Official Score Report to up to five programs for free, within a 48-hour period that starts when your Official Score is made available on mba.com (mba.com, 2026). You see the score before you choose, whether you tested at a test center or online. Each report carries the total and section scores of the exam you send it for. If your best Quantitative Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning and Data Insights scores came on different attempts, it also carries your GMAT Superscore, built from those section scores, with the exam date and delivery method of each, and you cannot leave it off (GMAC, 2026). Once a report has been sent, that cannot be cancelled or undone.

After the 48 hours, or for more than five programs, you buy additional score reports through your mba.com account, as long as the score is no more than five years old, and score reporting fees are non-refundable (mba.com, 2026). In the United States each costs US$35, whether you took the exam at a test center or online (mba.com, 2026). Our guide to what the GMAT costs covers the other fees.

GRE: Four Reports on Test Day

On test day you can designate up to four graduate institutions or fellowship sponsors to receive your scores as part of your test fee (ETS, 2026). With ScoreSelect, you choose for each of those four free reports whether to send your Most Recent scores or All of your GRE General Test scores from the last five years, or you can choose not to send scores at that time.

Scores sent after test day, or to more institutions, go as Additional Score Reports, US$40 per score recipient, which you can order at any time during the five years your scores are valid. After test day ScoreSelect also lets you send Any specific test administration. An order cannot be canceled, changed or refunded once submitted, and takes about 5 business days to process (ETS, 2026).

LSAT: A Report for Every Application

The LSAT includes no free score reports. A separate law school report is required for each law school application, at $45: some schools require a CAS Law School Report and others accept an LSAT-Only Law School Report. The Credential Assembly Service costs $219, on top of the $253 LSAT fee (LSAC, 2026). Your score is released only to you and to the law schools to which you have applied (LSAC, 2026).

What a school sees is fixed by LSAC rather than chosen by you: the score report carries the results of up to 12 reportable tests, absences and cancellations included, and the report LSAC sends each school includes your writing sample (LSAC, 2026). LSAC's fee waiver includes 6 law school reports in its first tier and 3 in its second (LSAC, 2026).

SAT: Four Reports Until Nine Days After

Each time you register for a weekend SAT, you can send your score to up to four organizations for free. You can choose them when you register or at any time until the test, and you have until nine days after the test to use or change them; after that, there is a fee (College Board, 2026). For SAT School Day, you choose your free score sends during exam setup or within three days after testing, as your school instructs. Each report beyond the free ones costs $15 (College Board, 2026).

You decide which test dates to send to each recipient, and scores from an entire SAT are sent together: you cannot send one test day's Math with another's Reading and Writing, though some colleges superscore once they have them. A college's own policy may ask to see all of your scores. With a fee waiver you can send as many score reports as you want, for free (College Board, 2026).

ACT: Four Colleges Named at Registration

ACT's registration price includes score reports for you, your high school and up to four colleges, if you give the colleges' codes when you register. Reports to a 5th and 6th college cost $20 each, requested online before the test date, and are refundable on written request if you do not test (ACT, 2026).

After registration, additional score reports cost $20 per test date, per report, and scores 3 or more years old add a $32 archive fee. A report is processed once your test is scored and all the results for your test option are available, and is usually sent the same day. If you have tested more than once since September 2016, you can send your superscore, the average of your highest section scores across test dates, instead of a single test's scores (ACT, 2026). The ACT fee waiver includes free additional score reports (ACT, 2026).

How to Use the Free Reports

Have your list ready before the window opens: for the ACT, when you register; for the GRE, by the end of your test; for the GMAT, within the 48 hours; and for the SAT, within the nine days. The GMAT and the GRE let you see a score before you choose: GMAC gives you your Official Score first, and ETS says that on test day, after viewing your scores at the test center, you decide whether and what to send (mba.com, 2026; ETS, 2026). On the SAT and the ACT the free reports are chosen before your scores exist: the nine days end before College Board releases most weekend scores, 2 to 4 weeks after test day (College Board, 2026), and ACT's four colleges are named when you register. If you would rather see a score before sending it, weigh the fee for a later report against the free one you would give up. Our guide to when scores come out has each exam's release timing.

Whichever test you are sending, the free trainers for the GMAT, GRE, LSAT, SAT and ACT run an adaptive round with no account needed, and report an estimated range from your practice, never a predicted score.
