A good PSAT/NMSQT score is one above the 50th percentile for your grade that also meets College Board's benchmarks for it. On College Board's percentile tables a total of 1200 is at the 90th nationally representative percentile for 11th graders and the 92nd for 10th graders, and the 11th grade benchmarks are 460 in Reading and Writing and 510 in Math (College Board, 2026). The test is scored 320 to 1520, with each section 160 to 760.

Which Percentile Should You Read?

College Board prints two for every score. Nationally representative percentiles come from a research study of U.S. students in 10th or 11th grade, weighted to represent all students in those grades whether or not they typically take the test. User group percentiles come from the actual scores of students who took the PSAT/NMSQT or the PSAT 10 in the past three school years (College Board, 2026). They can differ by several points: for an 11th grader, a 1100 is at the 79th nationally representative percentile but the 69th in the user group.

Your score report shows a third figure, the All Tester Percentile, which College Board bases on the past three cohorts of students who took the PSAT/NMSQT in 10th or 11th grade, and it gives each student the percentile for their own grade (College Board, 2026). That is the figure that describes you; the tables here show where a score sits across the scale.

College Board sets a benchmark for each section and grade, and in its words, "Students meeting or exceeding these benchmarks are considered on track to be college ready" (College Board, PSAT/NMSQT Understanding Scores, fall 2026). Meeting a benchmark and beating most of your grade are different bars: an 11th grader who scores exactly 460 in Reading and Writing is at the 42nd nationally representative percentile, and exactly 510 in Math is at the 62nd.

How Is the Selection Index Calculated?

National Merit Scholarship Corporation uses the NMSC Selection Index to screen students who enter its scholarship programs, and College Board describes the calculation this way: "Your score is calculated by doubling your Reading and Writing Score, adding it to your Math score, then dividing that sum by 10." The index runs from 48 to 228 and appears on PSAT/NMSQT score reports only (College Board, 2026). A student with 700 in Reading and Writing and 680 in Math has a Selection Index of (2 × 700 + 680) ÷ 10 = 208. Because Reading and Writing counts twice, ten more points there raise the index by 2, and ten more in Math raise it by 1.

How Does the PSAT Relate to the SAT?

College Board says the SAT Suite, from the PSAT 8/9 through the SAT, uses a common score scale for the total and section scores (College Board, 2026), so a PSAT/NMSQT score and an SAT score are read on the same scale: the PSAT/NMSQT tops out at 1520 and the SAT at 1600.
