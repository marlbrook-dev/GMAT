LSAC's free Reading Comprehension sample set has fourteen questions: seven on three single passages and seven on a comparative reading pair. The LSAT has one scored Reading Comprehension section, beside two scored Logical Reasoning sections (LSAC, 2026). This guide sets out the fourteen, what LSAC says about each, and where to find our guide to answering it.

What LSAC Says Reading Comprehension Tests

LSAC says the purpose of its Reading Comprehension questions is to measure the ability to read, with understanding and insight, examples of lengthy and complex materials similar to those commonly encountered in law school (LSAC, 2026). A section has four sets, each a selection followed by five to eight questions, with three or four single passages and either one comparative reading pair or none. The questions may ask about ten things, from the main idea or primary purpose to the impact of new information on claims or arguments in the selection, and our guide to approaching a Reading Comprehension passage lists all ten.

What All Fourteen Have in Common

  • Answer from the passage. LSAC's advice is to answer only on the basis of the information provided in the selection; you will not have to make inferences based on prior knowledge of the subject.
  • Read every choice. LSAC advises always reading all of the answer choices before selecting the best one.
  • Answer the question asked. LSAC warns against picking a choice simply because it is a true statement, which is the trap behind a main point answer that is only a detail.
  • Choose the best answer. LSAC's directions say more than one choice could conceivably answer some questions, and ask for the response that most accurately and completely answers the question.

How Should You Practice?

Work LSAC's fourteen samples first, since each comes with LSAC's own explanation, then move to full PrepTests, which our guide to free official practice tests lists. The LSAT trainer drills main idea, stated information, inference and structure questions on its own long passages, and our guide to the Logical Reasoning question types does for the other scored sections what this one does for reading.
