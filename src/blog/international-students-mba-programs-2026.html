International students make up more than a third of the middle program's MBA class, with a wide spread. Across 35 programs in our school library with a published figure for a class that entered in 2025 or 2026, international students are from 10 to 63 percent of the class, and the middle program's class is 37 percent international (school class profiles, Poets&Quants and the University of Miami, 2025 and 2026). Four of the 35 are at half or more, and seven are under 30 percent.

What Counts As International?

Schools do not all count the same students. Dartmouth Tuck's 34 percent includes dual citizens and permanent residents, Duke Fuqua counts students whose primary citizenship is outside the US, and Miami Herbert's 32 percent are the students who are not US citizens (school class profiles and the University of Miami, 2025 and 2026). A school that counts dual citizens can report a higher share than one that counts only students who are not US citizens, from the same class. Some profiles add the number of countries, which says more about the class's range: Stanford GSB's Class of 2027 comes from 64 countries (Stanford GSB, 2025).

A Change at One School

A single year's figure can move. Columbia's international enrollment declined from 46 percent in the class that entered in 2024 to 41 percent in the class that entered in 2025, the lowest in many years according to Poets&Quants' report on the class (Poets&Quants, 2025). If a school's share matters to you, look at more than one year of its profile before reading much into it.

How Should You Use These Numbers?

  • Check what a school counts as international before comparing two schools' figures.
  • Read the share beside the number of countries where a school gives both, and beside the rest of the profile: how to read an MBA class profile covers the numbers next to it.
  • If you are applying from outside the US, reading a class profile from outside the US covers the parts of a profile written for domestic applicants.
