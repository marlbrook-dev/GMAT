Two of the five big admissions tests can be taken at home. The GMAT is delivered online as well as at test centers, and the GRE at home is offered around the clock (mba.com, 2026; ETS, 2026). The LSAT went the other way: starting with the August 2026 administration, its multiple-choice portion is moving toward in-center testing for almost all test takers, though its writing section is still done online (LSAC, 2026). The SAT and the ACT are given only at schools and test sites (College Board, 2026; ACT, 2026). Here is what each option involves, and what can go wrong on the day.

Taking the GMAT Online

The GMAT exam delivered online is available in most locations, with the exception of Mainland China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea and Sudan, and proctoring support is currently offered only in English (mba.com, 2026). In the United States it costs US$300, against US$275 at a test center (mba.com, 2026); our guide to what the GMAT costs covers the rescheduling and score report fees. A voucher or fee waiver is tied to one format: GMAC's policies say one issued for the exam delivered online may not be used at a test center, and one for a test center may not be used online (GMAC, 2026).

The computer has to pass before you do. It must be a desktop or laptop, with no tablets, mobile devices, extra monitors or touchscreen use, and a stable connection of 2 Mbps down and up, with tethering to a mobile hotspot prohibited. You install GMAC's secure browser and run its system check ahead of time, because if your system does not meet the requirements on exam day, you cannot test and you forfeit the exam fee (mba.com, 2026).

The room is checked too. It must be a private, enclosed space with four walls, with the door behind you or visible to the proctor, and at check-in you use your webcam for a 360-degree scan of the room. For scratch work you can use a digital whiteboard on screen, a physical whiteboard no larger than 12 by 20 inches, or both; paper, pens and pencils are not allowed. You are asked to arrive 30 minutes early for check-in. The exam has one optional 10-minute break, after the first or the second section, and you leave your camera running while you are away (mba.com, 2026). Our guide to how long each test is sets out the sections around it.

Taking the GRE at Home

The GRE General Test at home is offered 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, everywhere the test is normally available, based on the country of your account address, and every at home test is monitored by a human proctor (ETS, 2026). ETS's fees page lists the General Test fee by region, $249 in most of the world, with no separate price for testing at home (ETS, 2026).

ETS's equipment and environment requirements are specific (ETS, 2026):

  • A desktop or laptop computer, not a tablet, Chromebook or mobile device, with no dual monitors, and the Chrome browser.
  • A second camera: a fully charged smartphone or tablet, set up beside you once the test starts.
  • Identity verification in the IDVaaS app at least 72 hours before the test.
  • A room where you are alone, with the doors closed and no glass walls, and a standard chair rather than a bed or couch. Food and drink are not allowed.
  • Notes on one small desktop whiteboard with an erasable marker, or one sheet of paper inside a transparent sheet protector. Pencils and pens are not permitted, and you show the proctor your notes erased at the end.

If you do not meet the requirements on test day, you cannot test and you receive neither a refund nor a free reschedule (ETS, 2026). At a test center none of this is your problem: ETS offers the test at more than 1,000 test centers in 160+ countries, available on a continuous basis in most regions (ETS, 2026).

Can You Take the LSAT at Home?

Only with an exception. Starting with the August 2026 LSAT, the multiple-choice portion is moving toward in-center testing for almost all test takers (LSAC, 2026). For the 2026-2027 administrations, LSAC considers remote testing for:

  • Test takers with certain disability-related accommodations that require remote testing.
  • Test takers stationed abroad on active duty in the U.S. military, and spouses and dependents stationed abroad with them.
  • Test takers in a U.S., U.S. territory or Canadian administration who are more than 180 miles or three hours' driving distance from a test center with available capacity.
  • Test takers whose domestic test center closes, for weather or other unforeseen reasons, and who cannot readily be moved to another center.

A distance request goes through the LSAT Status page in JD Services, from the day after registration closes until four days before test center scheduling closes, with documentation of the address you will test from. LSAC makes no exception for a preferred day or time, and if you are approved to test remotely, you are limited to that modality. Remote test takers must also use a smartphone as a second camera (LSAC, 2026). In person, you test at a Prometric test center, arriving up to 30 minutes early, with your phone, food and drinks stored in a locker (LSAC, 2026).

The writing section is the part you still do at home. LSAT Argumentative Writing is administered only online, on demand, through proctoring software installed on your own computer. Most test takers get 50 minutes, 15 for prewriting analysis and 35 for the essay. It opens eight days before your multiple-choice test, and the LSAT registration fee covers it (LSAC, 2026). Your score is not released until an approved writing sample is on file, so do it early; our guide to when scores come out explains the wait.

Can You Take the SAT at Home?

No. College Board says the digital SAT is administered in a school or a test center with a proctor present (College Board, 2026). What changed with the digital test is that you usually bring the computer. Bluebook, the testing app, runs on a Windows laptop or tablet, a Mac laptop, an iPad or a school-managed Chromebook, but not on a personal Chromebook or a phone, and College Board asks you to download it at least 30 days before test day (College Board, 2026).

If you have no device and cannot borrow one, College Board may be able to lend you one. You request it at least 30 days before test day, an adult who is not a family member verifies your eligibility, and an approved device is waiting at your test center; a request does not guarantee one (College Board, 2026). On a Saturday, test centers open at 7:45 a.m. and doors close at 8 a.m. unless your admission ticket says otherwise, and you cannot be admitted once testing has started. Your device needs a full charge, since it stays on for roughly 3 hours and an outlet is not guaranteed (College Board, 2026).

Can You Take the ACT at Home?

No. ACT says the online ACT is not available as a remote exam, and you take it at your chosen test center; an accommodation is the route for an alternate location such as a hospital (ACT, 2026). When you register you choose among three modes: paper, online on a test center device, or Bring Your Own Device, with your own computer or a school-managed computer brought to the test site (ACT, 2026).

If you pick your own device, bring it: test centers are not required to provide backups, and showing up without a device means you cannot test. Personal Chromebooks, Windows S Mode, iPads and tablets are not supported. You can change format until the late registration deadline, for a fee. Outside the United States, the ACT is only available online (ACT, 2026). Online testers may use scratch paper supplied by the testing site; on paper, you write in the test booklet instead (ACT, 2026).

Home or Test Center: How to Choose

Where you do have the choice, for the GMAT and the GRE, the test is the same either way; what differs is the risk you carry. At home, the equipment, the connection and the room are yours to get right, and both GMAC and ETS keep the fee if your setup fails on the day. At a test center, the risks are the journey and the appointment. If you test at home, run the test maker's system check on the computer you will actually use, well before test day, and practice your scratch work on the whiteboard you will use, since neither exam lets you take notes on regular paper at home.

Whichever you choose, the free trainers for the GMAT, GRE, LSAT, SAT and ACT run an adaptive round with no account needed, and report an estimated range from your practice, never a predicted score.
