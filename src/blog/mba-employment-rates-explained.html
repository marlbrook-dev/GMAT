An MBA employment rate is the share of graduates seeking a job who had one by a set point after graduation, and two choices change it: when it is counted, and whether it counts offers received or offers accepted. At UCLA Anderson, 74.7 percent of the Class of 2025 had accepted an offer by three months after graduation and 85 percent by six (UCLA Anderson, 2025). Same class, same school, about ten points apart.

What Does an MBA Employment Rate Measure?

Most schools report the share of graduates seeking employment who had an offer, or had accepted one, three months after graduation. Harvard Business School's is typical in form: 90 percent of students seeking employment had received an offer three months after graduation, for the Class of 2025 (HBS, 2025). Each part of that sentence is a choice. A different point in time, a different kind of offer, or a different group of graduates gives a different number from the same class.

Northwestern Kellogg, for example, reports that 90 percent of the Class of 2025 accepted roles within six months of graduation (Kellogg, 2025).

Offers Received or Offers Accepted?

A graduate who holds an offer but has not yet said yes counts as placed in one measure and not in the other, so the received rate is the higher of the two. Many reports print both:

Who Counts As Seeking Employment?

The rate is a share of the graduates seeking a job, not of the whole class. Maryland Smith's 77.3 percent is 34 of the 44 Class of 2025 graduates who were seeking employment (Smith, 2025), and Texas McCombs reports its Class of 2024 rate as a share of 184 students seeking employment (McCombs, 2024). Rates can also differ by work authorization: beside its three month figure, UNC Kenan-Flagler's report prints 91 percent for students with U.S. work authorization and 81 percent for students without (Kenan-Flagler, 2025). An international applicant should look for that split, and reading a class profile from outside the US covers the rest of the numbers worth checking.

How Do Our School Pages Show It?

Every employment rate on our school pages says when it was counted, at three months, six months, or another point the school names, beside the note that says what was counted. The SFN Score in our MBA rankings library counts only three month rates, so a six month figure is shown but never ranked against three month ones. How to read an MBA class profile applies the same questions to test scores and GPAs: which statistic, which class, and who is counted.
