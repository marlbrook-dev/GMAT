The ACT costs $70 in the United States, and that covers English, math and reading (ACT, 2026). The optional science section adds $5 and the optional writing essay $25, so the ACT with both costs $100. Outside the United States the ACT costs $188.50, or $223.50 with science and writing. In the United States every price includes score reports for you, your high school and up to four colleges. Here is every other ACT fee, and what missing a test costs.

What Does It Cost to Change or Miss the ACT?

A test date change, test center change or test form change costs $49, which is 70 percent of the $70 test fee (ACT, 2026). A test date change made during the late registration period for a national test date also carries the $42 late fee, $91 in all. Before the late deadline you can add or drop science or writing, and switch between paper, online and bring-your-own-device testing if a seat is available. You cannot change test centers after the published accommodations deadline; ACT points you to standby testing instead.

If you miss the test for any reason, including illness, late arrival or being turned away for lack of ID, you can request and pay for a test date change by the regular deadline for the new date. At $49 it costs less than registering again at $70, and registering for a new date instead of changing forfeits the original date's basic fee. If you do not test on another date at all, the registration fee and any additional fees are not refunded (ACT registration, 2026). One line to know before test day: once you break the seal on your test booklet, a test date change is no longer possible, even if you do not finish, and retesting means registering again and paying the full fee.

Can You Get a Refund for the ACT?

Mostly not. ACT's fees are nonrefundable unless it notes otherwise, and if you miss the test and do not move to another date, the registration fee and any added fees stay paid (ACT, 2026). The exceptions ACT lists:

  • The $75 standby fee is refunded if you are denied admission to the test center on test day, or if your registration was cancelled because no photo was provided.
  • The $20 for score reports to 5th and 6th college choices is refundable on written request if you do not test.
  • If you cannot test as a standby, the request does not carry over to another date or test center; ACT refunds the fees paid for it after all answer documents for that test are processed.

The SAT works differently: cancel by the Thursday before test day and College Board returns the registration fee, less a cancellation fee. Our guide to what the SAT costs sets out its fees.

How Much Do ACT Score Reports Cost?

In the United States every test option includes score reports for you, your high school and up to four colleges, if you give the colleges' codes when you register (ACT, 2026). Reports to 5th and 6th college choices cost $20 when requested online before the test date. Additional reports ordered after registration cost $20 each per test date, and scores 3 or more years old add a $32 archive fee that is not refunded. ACT processes a request once your test is scored and all results for your test option are available, and usually sends the report the same day. So the cheapest plan is to name your colleges when you register.

Two score services cost extra. ACT My Answer Key, a copy of your answers with the test questions and correct answers, costs $36 ordered before the test begins and $44 after, and is offered only on some test dates. Score verification costs $67 for the multiple-choice tests or the writing test, or $134 for both.

What Does the ACT Fee Waiver Cover?

ACT's fee waiver covers the registration fee for up to two ACT tests, plus the science and writing add-ons, so a student who qualifies pays nothing to take the ACT (ACT Fee Waiver Program, 2026). It also includes:

  • 1 year of free access to The Official ACT Self-Paced Course, Powered by Kaplan.
  • ACT My Answer Key at no extra cost.
  • Free additional score reports: the waiver covers a report to your high school and up to six colleges, and once scores are out you can send as many more as you need.
  • If you test with a fee waiver, a form asking colleges to waive or defer their application fees, which you send to each college rather than to ACT. Colleges may consider the request but are not obliged to grant it.

To qualify you must be in 11th or 12th grade, testing in the United States, a US territory or Puerto Rico, and meet at least one indicator of economic need: enrollment in the federal free or reduced-price lunch program at school, a program for economically disadvantaged students such as GEAR UP or Upward Bound, living in a foster home, as a ward of the state or homeless, a family on low-income public assistance or in federally subsidized public housing, or a family income at or below the USDA levels for free or reduced-price lunch. Your school counselor determines whether you are eligible (ACT, 2026).

A few rules decide what a waiver is worth. It counts as used once you register, whether or not you test. If you miss the test you can move it to a later date, but you pay the $49 change fee. You can use at most two, a waiver cannot refund a fee you have already paid, and it does not pay for other fees, products or services. ACT's supply of waivers is limited (ACT fee waiver requirements, 2026).

How Does the ACT's Cost Compare With the SAT's?

In the United States the two cost about the same: the ACT is $70 and the SAT $68 (College Board, 2026). Outside the United States the ACT costs $188.50 and the SAT $111 with its international fee. The extras differ more. The SAT's first 4 score reports are free if ordered within 9 days of the test and $15 each after, where additional ACT reports cost $20 each; and cancelling the SAT in time returns part of the fee, where the ACT keeps it.
