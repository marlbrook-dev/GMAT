Most full-time MBA students arrive with about five years of work experience. Across 38 programs in our school library with a published figure for a class that entered in 2025 or 2026, the average runs from 17.1 months at College of Charleston's one-year MBA to 6 years at seven programs, the middle program's average is 5.1 years, and 29 of the 38 sit between 4.9 and 6 years (school class profiles and Poets&Quants, 2025 and 2026). None of those averages is a requirement: Wharton's class profile says there is no minimum or maximum years of experience to apply (Wharton, 2025).

Is There a Minimum Amount of Work Experience?

Not at the schools that say so. Wharton's class profile states that while its students average 5 years of work experience, there is no minimum or maximum years of experience to apply (Wharton, 2025). The ranges schools print point the same way: NYU Stern's Class of 2027 runs from 0 to 14 years, with 98 percent of the class having work experience, and Colorado Leeds's Class of 2028 from 0 to 16 years (school class profiles, 2025 and 2026). Where a school prints its middle 80 percent, about one student in ten sits below the bottom of it: below 3 years at BU Questrom and Texas McCombs, below 1.6 years at Rochester Simon (school class profiles, 2025).

Programs Built for Fewer Years

Four programs in the table average under four years, and they show how much the answer depends on the kind of program. College of Charleston's one-year MBA averages 17.1 months, NC State Poole's class that entered in fall 2026 averages 2.32 years, UC Riverside's averages 38 months, and the middle 80 percent of Boston College Carroll's Class of 2028 runs from 0 to 5.9 years (school class profiles, 2025 and 2026). If you have a year or two of experience, compare yourself with programs like these rather than with the 5 to 6 year averages at the top of the table.

How Should You Use These Numbers?

  • Read a school's range or middle 80 percent where it prints one, not only its average.
  • Check which class a figure describes and whether the school counts full-time or professional experience; Cornell Johnson and Wisconsin say which they count.
  • Set the rest of the profile beside it: average GPA for MBA programs and how to read an MBA class profile cover the numbers that sit next to experience.
