A good GMAT score in 2026 is a score at or above the number your target schools report for their most recent class. There is no universal cutoff. At the most selective MBA programs, Class of 2027 figures cluster between 675 and 689 on the GMAT Focus Edition, which is scored from 205 to 805. At most other programs, a good score sits well below that. Good means competitive for the specific schools on your list, nothing more.

One complication makes 2026 unusual. Schools still report two exams side by side: the retired GMAT Classic, scored 200 to 800, and the current GMAT Focus Edition, scored 205 to 805 in 10-point steps, with three sections each scored 60 to 90 (mba.com, GMAC, 2026). The numbers look similar but sit on different scales, so a Classic 700 and a Focus 705 are very different results. This guide labels every number by edition and compares editions by percentile.

Why does a good score depend on your schools?

Admissions committees read a GMAT score against the class they are building, not against an absolute standard. A 645 on the Focus Edition would raise no questions at many strong programs, yet it sits 30 points below the MIT Sloan Focus median of 675 (MIT Sloan Class of 2027 profile, 2025). The reverse is also true: spending months chasing a 705 Focus score for a program whose incoming class scores far lower is time better spent on essays, applications, or work. Before you set a target, pull the class profile for every school on your list and write down the number it actually reports.

Pay attention to which statistic a school publishes. An average can be pulled up or down by a handful of extreme scores. A median is the literal middle of the class: half the students scored at or below it. The four schools below split evenly on this point. Stanford and Wharton report averages, while MIT Sloan and Harvard Business School report medians.

How do Classic and Focus scores compare?

By percentile, never by raw number. GMAC's official concordance tables place a Classic 700 and a Focus 655 at the same 90.5th percentile (GMAC concordance tables), so if older advice said to aim for 700, the equivalent ambition on today's exam is a Focus 655, not a Focus 705. How do you set a personal target score?

A workable method takes three steps.

  1. Find the anchor. Take the highest median or average among the schools on your list. If a school reports only Classic figures, convert them to a Focus equivalent by percentile before doing anything else.
  2. Add a margin of 10 to 20 points. Aiming 10 to 20 Focus points above the anchor buys two kinds of insurance. The first is test-day variance: few people match their best practice result on the real exam. The second is application balance: a score above the class center lets the rest of your file carry less weight, which helps if your GPA, work history, or undergraduate record is a relative weakness.
  3. Check the section picture. The three Focus sections, Quantitative Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning, and Data Insights, are equally weighted and each scored 60 to 90 (mba.com, GMAC, 2026). A lopsided profile can undercut a solid total, especially a weak quant result aimed at a finance-heavy program. Train the weak section rather than squeezing more points from the strong one.

For a school reporting a Focus median of 675, this method points to a personal target of 685 to 695. That is demanding but not arbitrary. It is anchored to a published number rather than to folklore about what a good GMAT score is supposed to be.

When does a lower score still work?

Medians are not floors. Several situations regularly produce admitted students below the published center of a class.

  • The rest of the file is strong. The published ranges prove it. Stanford's Focus range starts at 615, which is 74 points below its 689 Focus average (Stanford GSB Class of 2027 profile, 2025). Those students were admitted on the strength of everything else in their applications.
  • You bring the evidence the score usually stands in for. A quantitative degree with a strong record, or clearly analytical work experience, can answer the question a test score is meant to answer.
  • The school accepts an alternative test that suits you better. At Harvard Business School, 44 percent of the Class of 2027 submitted a GRE, more than submitted either GMAT edition, with a median of 164 Quant and 164 Verbal (HBS Class of 2027 profile, 2025). If the GMAT format works against you, read our GMAT versus GRE comparison before committing to either exam.

The sober version of this advice: a below-median score does not end an application, but it shifts weight onto parts of the file that are harder to change late in the process. If focused practice can close the gap, closing it is usually the cheaper path.

What should you do with these numbers?

Treat the chart above as a map, not a verdict. Pick your schools first. Convert every published figure to the Focus scale by percentile. Set one target 10 to 20 points above the highest number on your list, then let a timed diagnostic tell you how far away you are. A good GMAT score in 2026 is simply the one that makes your school list realistic, and that number is different for every applicant.
