All five tests check a photo ID against the name you registered under, and none accepts a photocopy. Only the LSAT takes an expired ID, and only within 3 months of the test (LSAC, 2026). The SAT and the ACT accept a school ID, which the LSAT refuses (College Board, 2026; ACT, 2026; LSAC, 2026). The GMAT and the SAT want a passport if you test outside your own country (mba.com, 2026; College Board, 2026). Here is each test's rule, and what happens if your ID fails it.

GMAT: Your Name Must Match Exactly

Your ID must be an original physical document, since copies are not accepted, and valid and unexpired, with your full name and a recent, recognizable photograph. It must match the name in your mba.com account and your registration, and it must meet the rules for your testing location, which vary by country; some locations require a passport regardless of citizenship. If you test outside your country of citizenship, as you entered it when you registered, you must present a valid passport (mba.com, 2026).

The first and last name on your registration must exactly match the ones on your ID, and GMAC lists the differences that can keep you out: spelling, missing names, reversed names, additional names and nicknames. Arrive without acceptable identification and you are not admitted, your appointment is marked a no-show, your fee is forfeited and a new appointment costs the full fee; exceptions cannot be granted at the testing site or on exam day. If you lack a required passport or acceptable ID, you can ask Customer Care for a review, but approval is not guaranteed and must be received at least five business days before your appointment (mba.com, 2026). GRE: Original, Government Issued and Signed

The first and last name you register with, and their spelling, must exactly match the name printed on your ID, excluding accents. ETS asks for your entire first and last names, with no nickname and no initial in place of a first name, and warns that if they do not match you will not be able to take the test and your fees will not be refunded (ETS, 2026).

With few exceptions, each ID document must be an original (no photocopies) and a physical ID, since a digital ID on a phone is not acceptable except for some digital IDs in the Republic of Korea. It must be a government-issued national, state or province identity card, bear your first and last name exactly as you registered, include a recent, recognizable photograph and your signature, and be unexpired. A student ID counts only as a supplemental ID, which you may be asked for if the test administrator questions your primary ID or it lacks your name, photograph or signature, and a supplemental ID cannot resolve a name that does not match. ETS lists documents it never accepts, among them an international driver's license, an international student ID, a birth certificate, a Social Security card, an employee ID card and any credit or debit card. If you have been granted political asylum, have refugee status or otherwise cannot meet the requirements, contact ETS's Office of Testing Integrity at least 7 days before registering, and get its approval before you register (ETS, 2026). Testing at home adds an identity check in the IDVaaS app, which ETS asks you to complete at least 72 hours before the test; our guide to testing at home covers it.

LSAT: Passport, or US or Canadian Photo ID

To take the LSAT, including LSAT Argumentative Writing, you must present a physical, valid international passport or a physical, valid government-issued photo ID issued by the United States, a US territory or Canada. The ID must be current or have expired within 3 months of your test date, and it must show a recent, recognizable photo, your first and last names and your date of birth. The first and last name must exactly match the legal name on your LSAC account. Common acceptable IDs include a passport book, a driver's license, a state or province ID card, a US Permanent Resident Card, a Canadian Permanent Resident Card and certain Canadian health care benefit cards (LSAC, 2026).

LSAC does not accept a student ID, an employee ID (even a government employee's), a Social Security or Social Insurance card, a birth certificate, a credit card with or without a photo, a photocopied ID, an ID expired for more than 3 months, or a US military ID card, which by law cannot be photographed and so cannot be used for online testing. If you are unsure about your ID, call LSAC at least 14 days before your registered test date. Test takers in North America who cannot obtain the required ID can ask LSAC to approve alternate documents, but only before the registration deadline for their test date (LSAC, 2026).

SAT: Government or Current School ID

Test center staff compare your admission ticket and photo ID with the roster, and you cannot be admitted if anything does not match: a nickname on your ticket and your full name on your ID is enough to keep you out. Your ID must be a valid, unexpired photo ID issued by a government or by the school you currently attend, and it must be a physical, original document, since digital IDs are not accepted. It must bear your full legal name exactly as on your admission ticket, and a recent photo that matches both your appearance on test day and the photo you gave when you registered (College Board, 2026).

Acceptable examples include a driver's license or non-driver ID card, a current-school student ID, a passport or US Global Entry card, and a military or national ID card. School IDs from the prior school year are valid through December 31 of the current calendar year. Students under 21 testing at US centers can instead use the SAT Student ID Form, prepared by their school or, if homeschooled, a notary. If you will be 21 or older on test day, only an official government-issued photo ID counts. Traveling to another country to test means using a passport, and some countries have their own lists. Staff are not required to hold your seat while you fetch an ID; you need to keep your ID with you all day, breaks included. A name change needs a call to Customer Service at least 30 days before the test (College Board, 2026).

ACT: A Hard Plastic Photo ID, or ACT's Form

ACT asks for an original, valid photo ID issued by a city, state, federal agency or your school, on a hard plastic card, since paper and electronic IDs are not accepted. The name on it must match your registration, and your photo must clearly identify you. If you have no current official photo ID, you must present the ACT Student Identification Form with a photo, fully completed by a school official or a notary public; a relative cannot complete it (ACT, 2026).

ACT will not admit you with any other form of ID, and its list of unacceptable ones runs long: a birth certificate, a Social Security card, credit or bank cards even with a photo, an ID issued by an employer, a report card or transcript, a learner's permit or temporary license without a photo, photocopies, a web page with your photo, and personal recognition by anyone, test center staff, teachers and parents included (ACT, 2026).

Check Your ID When You Register

The same few documents fail on every test that lists them. The GRE, the LSAT, the SAT and the ACT all refuse a birth certificate, a Social Security card, an employee ID and a credit card, even one with a photo (ETS, 2026; LSAC, 2026; College Board, 2026; ACT, 2026). The fix for most ID problems is also the same: register under the name exactly as it is printed on the ID you will bring, and check that ID's expiry date against your test date before you book. An ID problem found on test day costs the whole fee on the GMAT, the GRE and the SAT (mba.com, 2026; ETS, 2026; College Board, 2026).

Whichever test you are preparing for, the free trainers for the GMAT, GRE, LSAT, SAT and ACT run an adaptive round with no account needed, and report an estimated range from your practice, never a predicted score.
