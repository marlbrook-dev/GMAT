Four of the five tests have math, and they do not test the same math. The GMAT's Quantitative Reasoning section measures algebra and arithmetic (GMAC, 2026). The GRE adds geometry and data analysis but, in ETS's words, goes no higher than a second course in algebra (ETS, 2026). The SAT and the ACT reach further, into trigonometry on the SAT and functions and statistics on both (College Board, 2026; ACT, 2026). The LSAT is left out here because its four sections are Logical Reasoning and Reading Comprehension (LSAC, 2026). Here is what each test's math covers, in its maker's own words.

GMAT: Algebra and Arithmetic, Without a Calculator

The GMAT's Quantitative Reasoning section consists of 21 multiple-choice Problem Solving questions, to be completed in 45 minutes. GMAC says it measures algebraic and arithmetic foundational knowledge and how this knowledge is applied to solve problems (GMAC, 2026). There is no calculator in that section; the on-screen calculator is available only in Data Insights (GMAC, 2026).

Data Insights is where the GMAT's data questions sit: 20 multiple-choice questions in 45 minutes, drawn from five question types, data sufficiency, multi-source reasoning, table analysis, graphics interpretation and two-part analysis (GMAC, 2026). GRE: Four Content Areas, Up to a Second Course in Algebra

ETS assesses GRE Quantitative Reasoning in four content areas. Arithmetic covers properties of integers, such as divisibility, factorization, primes and remainders, along with exponents and roots, percent, ratio, rate and sequences. Algebra covers factoring, functions, linear and quadratic equations and inequalities, simultaneous equations, word problems and coordinate geometry. Geometry covers lines, circles, triangles, quadrilaterals and other polygons, 3-dimensional figures, area, perimeter, volume, the Pythagorean theorem and angles; the ability to construct proofs is not tested. Data analysis covers descriptive statistics, tables and graphs, elementary and conditional probability, probability distributions including the normal distribution, and counting methods; inferential statistics is not tested (ETS, 2026).

ETS also sets a ceiling. The content is high school mathematics and statistics at a level generally no higher than a second course in algebra, and it does not include trigonometry, calculus or other higher-level mathematics. Many of the questions are word problems, which must be translated and modeled mathematically (ETS, 2026).

SAT: Four Domains, Weighted Toward Algebra

The SAT Math section is 44 questions in two 35-minute modules, 70 minutes in all (College Board, 2026). College Board divides it into four content domains, and publishes how many questions each gets (College Board, 2026):

ACT: Higher Math, Plus Essential Skills

ACT's mathematics test is 45 questions in 50 minutes (ACT, 2026). ACT reports it in categories: Preparing for Higher Math, 80% of the test, divided into Number and Quantity (10% to 12%), Algebra (17% to 20%), Functions (17% to 20%), Geometry (17% to 20%) and Statistics and Probability (12% to 15%); and Integrating Essential Skills, 20% (ACT, 2026). A further category, Modeling, is scored across the other categories (ACT, 2026). Each reporting category on your score report shows the number of questions in it and how many you answered correctly (ACT, 2026).

Which Test's Math Suits You

If you are choosing between the GMAT and the GRE for business school, the math is one real difference: the GMAT's Quantitative Reasoning section is algebra and arithmetic with no calculator, while the GRE brings in geometry and data analysis and gives you an on-screen calculator throughout Quantitative Reasoning (GMAC, 2026; ETS, 2026).
