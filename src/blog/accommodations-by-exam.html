All five tests offer extra testing time and other accommodations to test takers with disabilities, and all five need them approved before test day. What differs is the order of the steps and how long they take. The GMAT and the GRE want approval before you book, since accommodations cannot be added to an appointment you already have (mba.com, 2026; ETS, 2026). The LSAT wants you registered first and takes requests only until its registration deadline, and since June 2026 the ACT has used its registration deadline too (LSAC, 2026; ACT, 2026). College Board says SAT approval can take up to seven weeks (College Board, 2026). Here is each process, and how far ahead to start.

GMAT: Get Approved Before You Book

GMAC's accommodations may include additional testing time, an additional rest break, a medical device in the testing room, a reader/recorder who reads the questions and records your answers, enlarged font, adjustable contrast and a sign language interpreter. To apply, read the GMAT Supplement for Test Takers with Disabilities, create an mba.com account, gather documentation as the Supplement describes it for your disability type, and submit the Apply for Accommodations form on GMAC's accommodations page; if no email confirmation arrives, ask for support. If you have received accommodations before, a confirmation of prior accommodations review form may be an option when you are seeking 50% additional time, an additional rest break, or both (mba.com, 2026).

GMAC says every effort will be made to respond to accommodation requests within 16 to 20 business days of receipt (mba.com, 2026). Do not book a standard appointment while you wait. Accommodations cannot be changed or added for an existing appointment, and if you book a standard appointment before your accommodations are approved, GMAC cannot apply them to that session and your registration fee is forfeited; nor will it waive or refund rescheduling or cancellation fees. Once you are approved, the approval email tells you how to schedule an accommodated appointment (mba.com, 2026; mba.com, 2026). Testing outside the United States does not rule it out: GMAC says you can still request accommodations based on a disability (mba.com, 2026).

GRE: Allow About Six Weeks Before You Schedule

ETS offers extended testing time of 25% (time and one-quarter), 50% (time and one-half) or 100% (double time), extra breaks that are not counted as testing time, and a long list of other accommodations, from screen magnification and JAWS to a human reader, a scribe, braille and recorded audio. You must submit your request and have your accommodations approved by ETS Disability Services before you can schedule your test, and accommodations cannot be applied to a test that has already been scheduled (ETS, 2026).

ETS's accommodations page says documentation review takes approximately 6 weeks once your request and complete paperwork have been received, and it can be another 6 weeks if you have to send more documentation. Its 2026-27 Bulletin Supplement gives shorter spans: approximately four to six weeks for the review, and another two to four if more documentation is requested. You can apply through your ETS account, or by email, mail or courier service, and a health-related need to bring equipment, beverages or snacks into the testing room, or to take extra or extended breaks, goes through the same process (ETS, 2026).

Some requests need no disability documentation. If you have a learning disability, ADHD or one of the other disabilities the Supplement lists, and are asking for 50% extended time or less, extra breaks, or both, a valid Certification of Eligibility: Accommodations History, which verifies your use of accommodations in college or at work, can stand in for it (ETS, 2026).

LSAT: Register First, Then Request by the Deadline

LSAC takes accommodation requests online only, through JD Services, and only once you are registered for the test date you want them for. The accommodation request deadline is always the same as the registration deadline for that LSAT, and there are no exceptions: the online form stops accepting requests for a test date once its deadline has passed (LSAC, 2026). LSAC gives no review time: asked how long a decision takes, it says it reviews requests as quickly as possible, and that a request for a later test may wait behind the deadlines for earlier ones (LSAC, 2026).

A second LSAT is simpler. Registered test takers are automatically approved for the accommodations they were approved for on their last LSAT, with no new request or documentation, though some must be requested again, among them a paper-and-pencil format or a modality exception approved before August 2025, and assistive technology received before the August 2026 LSAT. Some items need no request at all, among them medication, non-tinted eyeglasses, hearing aids, a cane and a wheelchair, though a Bluetooth-enabled medical device must be approved as an accommodation. Test takers approved for 100% additional time or more can ask, by the same deadline, for five-minute breaks after sections 1 and 3 on top of the 10-minute intermission after section 2 (LSAC, 2026).

For the 2026-2027 testing cycle LSAC is moving almost all test takers into test centers, and the remote testing exceptions it will consider include candidates whose accommodations require a separate room in a test center (LSAC, 2026). SAT: Start Seven Weeks Ahead, Usually Through Your School

College Board's Services for Students with Disabilities says the approval process can take up to seven weeks, so start well before the test date, and that approval can take an additional seven weeks if more documentation is required or a request is resubmitted. It cannot review a request until all required documentation is received, and working through your school is usually easier and more efficient (College Board, 2026). Families can also send a paper request without going through the school (College Board, 2026). A plan at school is not enough on its own: even students with an IEP or 504 plan need to request accommodations for the SAT (College Board, 2026).

Each SAT date has its own deadline for the request and the required documentation: January 15, 2027 for the March 6, 2027 SAT, March 12 for May 1, and April 16 for June 5. Accommodations approved less than 14 days before test day, or requested after the deadline, may not be in place when you test, but if a decision comes too late for one test, you can use the approved accommodations on later College Board tests (College Board, 2026). For a temporary condition such as a broken arm, College Board says a request is probably not the answer, and that when possible it is best to wait until the condition has resolved (College Board, 2026).

ACT: Through Your School, by the Registration Deadline

ACT accommodations must be approved before you test, and you request them with a school official, since they should be similar to the accommodations you receive in school. Link your high school to your MyACT account, register, and answer Yes when asked whether you need accommodations; then forward the email you receive to your school official with a completed Consent to Release Information form, and the school submits the request. Registering first is not required, but ACT strongly recommends it. If your school will not submit a request, you can ask ACT for an exception with its Exceptions Statement Form, and homeschooled and non-enrolled students follow a separate process (ACT, 2026).

Starting with the June 2026 test event, the accommodations deadline is the regular registration deadline, and every request, appeals included, must be in by it: January 22 for the February 27, 2027 ACT, March 5 for April 10, and May 7 for June 12. ACT says review times vary with the volume of requests and how complete the documentation is. Decisions go to your school, and ACT suggests contacting the school if you have heard nothing after 10 business days. Some accommodations, such as time and one-half in a single session, can be given at national test centers; others, such as testing over more than one day, are given through ACT Special testing, now a nine-day window that includes two full weekends, usually at your own school (ACT, 2026).

Will Schools See That You Had Accommodations?

The makers of the GMAT, the LSAT and the ACT each say their score reports do not show it; the ETS and College Board pages cited here do not address it. GMAC says score reports do not indicate whether a test was taken with accommodations, and it does not share disability status with any schools (mba.com, 2026). LSAC does not annotate the score reports of test takers who take the LSAT with accommodations, extended time included, and does not tell law schools that a candidate requested or tested with them (LSAC, 2026). ACT does not indicate the use of accommodations on score reports sent to colleges (ACT, 2026).

Plan Backward From Your Test Date

Count back from the date you want, not forward from today. For the GMAT, allow 16 to 20 business days before you book; for the GRE, about six weeks before you schedule; for the SAT, seven weeks, and seven more if more documentation is needed; and for the LSAT and the ACT, treat the registration deadline as the last day to ask (mba.com, 2026; ETS, 2026; College Board, 2026; LSAC, 2026; ACT, 2026).
