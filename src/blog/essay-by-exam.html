Of the five tests, only the GRE scores an essay at every sitting. The LSAT requires one but does not score it, the ACT offers one as an option, the SAT gives one only on school days in states that require it, and the GMAT has none at all. Here is what each test asks you to write, how long you get, and what happens to the result, in each test maker's own words.

GMAT: No Essay in the Focus Edition

The GMAT Focus Edition is three 45-minute sections, Quantitative Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning and Data Insights, with 64 questions in 2 hours 15 minutes and no writing assessment (GMAC, 2026). The older GMAT Exam, 10th Edition had one: a report of a 10th Edition score still includes a copy of its Analytical Writing Assessment essay (GMAC, 2026). Our guide to what changed in the Focus Edition covers the rest.

GRE: One Issue Essay, Always First

GRE Analytical Writing is one Analyze an Issue task in 30 minutes, and it always comes first, with the Verbal and Quantitative sections in any order after it (ETS, 2026). It is scored 0 to 6 in half-point increments, alongside Verbal and Quantitative Reasoning on their 130 to 170 scales (ETS, 2026).

Your essay travels with your scores. Essay responses from each administration you send are available to your score recipients in the ETS Data Manager (ETS, 2026), and a score review of the Analytical Writing measure costs $60 (ETS, 2026).

LSAT: An Unscored Essay You Take Remotely

LSAT Argumentative Writing is an unscored writing sample, administered separately from the multiple-choice sections and delivered only online, through remote-proctoring software installed on your own computer. You get 15 minutes for prewriting analysis, which you may move past after five, and then 35 minutes to write your essay (LSAC, 2026).

Unscored does not mean optional. LSAC releases your scores only once an approved writing sample is on file, and a sample can take up to three weeks to process (LSAC, 2026). The law school report includes your LSAT scores and your writing sample (LSAC, 2026). Our LSAT format and scoring guide covers the rest of the test.

SAT: An Essay Only in Some States

The optional SAT Essay on weekend tests was discontinued in 2021. The Essay section is now only available in certain states where it is required as part of SAT School Day administrations; where it is included, it comes after the Reading and Writing and Math sections and adds 50 minutes (College Board, 2024). You read a passage of 650 to 750 words and explain how its author builds an argument, rather than giving your own view, and the Essay score is reported with your other section scores from that day (College Board, 2024).

ACT: An Optional 40-Minute Essay

The ACT's writing test is optional and adds 40 minutes to the test, with five minutes to relax and prepare before the essay (ACT, 2026). It is scored on a 2 to 12 scale across four domains: Ideas and Analysis, Development and Support, Organization, and Language Use and Conventions (ACT, 2026).

The writing score counts toward your ELA score, the average of English, reading and writing if taken, but not toward the Composite or the superscore (ACT, 2026). Scores for a test with writing take longer: five to eight weeks, against two to eight for multiple choice alone (ACT, 2026). Our ACT format guide has the rest of the test.

Which Essay Counts for You

For graduate school the answer is fixed by the test: every GRE carries its Analytical Writing score, every LSAT needs its writing sample on file before the scores come out, and the GMAT has nothing to write (ETS, 2026; LSAC, 2026; GMAC, 2026). For college, the ACT's essay is your choice and the SAT's depends on your state, and ACT advises checking each school's own score policy (ACT, 2026).
