GMAT scores are valid for five years and can be reported for up to 10 (mba.com, 2023). GRE scores are reportable for 5 years following the test date (ETS, 2026). An LSAT score is reportable for up to five testing years after the testing year in which it was earned, and scores from before July 2021 no longer count for law school admission (LSAC, 2026). Neither College Board nor ACT gives its scores an expiry date: College Board archives SAT scores a year after a student leaves high school and stops testing, and ACT charges an archive fee to send scores 3 or more years old (College Board, 2026; ACT, 2026). Here is each rule, and what it means for when you test.

How Long Are GMAT Scores Valid?

Five years. GMAC says GMAT scores are valid for five years and available for reporting for up to 10 years, and that results over 10 years old are not available (mba.com, 2023). The same five years apply to both editions: when it announced the GMAT Focus Edition, GMAC said all GMAT exam scores, from the previous edition and from the Focus Edition alike, continue to be valid for five years (GMAC, 2023). The two editions are scored on different scales, so compare them by percentile, never by the number. Our guide to the average GMAT score by MBA program shows how schools report both.

How Long Are GRE Scores Valid?

GRE scores are reportable for 5 years following your test date (ETS, 2026). ETS's page works out the exact last date for you from your test date, and once the test date is more than five years ago, the scores can no longer be reported. Within the five years you can order additional score reports at any time, for $40 per recipient, and choose to send your most recent scores, all of them, or any of the tests you took in that period (ETS, 2026).

How Long Are LSAT Scores Valid?

An LSAT result is reportable for up to five testing years after the testing year in which it was earned (LSAC, 2026). LSAT scores earned before July 2021 are not considered valid for law school admission and are not included in your score report. You can still order an Official Candidate LSAT Score Report of them for your own records. The score report carries the results of up to 12 reportable tests, absences and cancellations included, so the report shows your whole recent record, not only your best score. If you are thinking of testing again, our guide to how many times you can take each exam covers LSAC's limits.

Do SAT Scores Expire?

College Board gives SAT scores no expiry date (College Board, 2026). Once you have left high school and have not tested for a year, it archives your scores, along with some of the information you gave about yourself when you tested. You can still send archived scores to yourself or to colleges and scholarship programs, paying a retrieval fee on top of each report's fee: the retrieval fee is $35 (College Board, 2026). Scores older than 2005 are no longer accessible, and College Board says it is not obligated to keep older scores.

College Board also cautions that score reports sent to colleges five or more years after a test date may be less valid predictors of college performance than more recent scores, because knowledge and skills change over that time. It adds that SAT scores from before 2016 should be read with care, with its concordance tables, because the scale and the content changed.

Do ACT Scores Expire?

ACT's pages give no expiry date, but older scores cost more to send (ACT, 2026). Each score report ordered after registration costs $20 per test date, and scores 3 or more years old add a $32 archive fee, which is nonrefundable and which ACT says covers the cost of maintaining the data. If your ACT account was created before September 2011, only scores from tests registered through MyACT appear in it, and ACT has a request form for scores from before September 2011.

What This Means for When You Test

For the GMAT, the GRE and the LSAT, the window is fixed by the test maker, so a score has to be reportable when you apply, not only when you test. Count forward from your test date to the last application you expect to send, including any you might send a year or more later. For the SAT and the ACT, no test maker window applies, and it is each college's own policy that decides how old a score it will take; check the admissions page of every college on your list.

Whichever exam you take, the free trainers for the GMAT, GRE, LSAT, SAT and ACT run an adaptive round with no account needed, and report an estimated range from your practice, never a predicted score.
