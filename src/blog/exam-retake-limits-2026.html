The GMAT and the GRE each allow up to five attempts in any rolling 12 months. The GMAT asks for at least 16 calendar days between attempts and the GRE for 21 (mba.com, 2026; ETS, 2026). The LSAT allows five attempts since July 2021 and seven in all (LSAC, 2026). The SAT and the ACT set no limit (College Board, 2026; ACT, 2026). Here is each rule as its test maker states it, with what counts as an attempt.

How Many Times Can You Take the GMAT?

Up to five times within a rolling 12-month period, and you must wait at least 16 calendar days between appointments (mba.com, 2026). GMAC's example: take the exam on June 1 and the earliest you can test again is June 17. The 16 days apply whether you tested online or at a test center, whether you finished the exam, and whether your score was canceled.

Online and test center exams share one limit: two online exams and three at a test center in 12 months make five attempts. Exams with canceled scores count toward the five. Appointments canceled before the exam, and missed appointments, do not. GMAC grants exceptions only in limited circumstances, through a request to GMAC Customer Care with your appointment history and your reason. Its policy page states no cap beyond the five in 12 months. If you are weighing a second attempt, our GMAT retake decision framework walks through when one is worth it.

How Many Times Can You Take the GRE?

Once every 21 days, up to five times within any continuous rolling 12-month period, which ETS defines as 365 days (ETS, 2026). The limit applies even if you canceled your scores on a test taken earlier, so a canceled sitting still uses one of the five. ETS also says your score report arrives 8 to 10 days after your test date, which matters when you plan a retake around an application deadline.

How Many Times Can You Take the LSAT?

LSAC sets two limits (LSAC, 2026). You may take the LSAT five times within the current reportable score period, which LSAC dates from July 2021, and seven times in total over a lifetime. Tests taken before September 2019 do not count toward either number, and neither do the May, June, July and August 2020 LSAT-Flex tests.

Canceled scores count against both limits, including scores canceled with the Score Preview option. Absences and withdrawals do not. A test taker who has already scored 180, the top of the scale, within the current reportable score period may not take the LSAT again. LSAC estimates that the policy affects less than 1 percent of LSAT takers, and it considers exemptions for significant and extenuating circumstances, requested by email to TTL@LSAC.org with a detailed explanation.

How Many Times Can You Take the SAT?

As many times as you want (College Board, 2026). College Board recommends taking the SAT at least twice, in the spring of junior year and the fall of senior year, and says most students get a higher score the second time. It also notes that many colleges superscore, combining a student's best section scores from different test dates, and that most colleges consider a student's highest SAT score. Our guide to what counts as a good SAT score sets out the percentiles.

How Many Times Can You Take the ACT?

There is no limit, and ACT says that on average it takes students 2 to 3 attempts to reach their testing goals (ACT, 2026). You cannot retake a single section; each attempt is a full test. What you get instead is the ACT superscore, the average of your best English, math and reading scores across attempts, which ACT reports automatically once you have tested more than once. To retake, you sign up for any national ACT test date. Our ACT score calculator works out a Composite and a superscore from your section scores.

Which Attempts Do Not Count?

  • GMAT: appointments canceled before the exam, and appointments you missed (mba.com, 2026).
  • GRE: ETS's page lists none, and says the limit applies even if you canceled your scores (ETS, 2026).
  • LSAT: absences, withdrawals, tests taken before September 2019, and the May to August 2020 LSAT-Flex tests (LSAC, 2026).

How Many Times Should You Plan to Take It?

The limits are ceilings, not plans. Of the pages cited here, only College Board's and ACT's say how many attempts to expect: College Board recommends at least two SAT sittings, and ACT says students take the ACT 2 to 3 times on average (College Board, 2026; ACT, 2026).
